## Context and problem statement

ADR-0040 made Ads read-only because moving money needs its own risk model. The owner then said on 2026-11-01 that users should **run ads from Ludy**, with full campaigns ("Chiến dịch đầy đủ"), as Hootsuite Ads does. Running an ad spends the owner's money on a platform, can reach the public, and cannot be undone once spent. What has to be decided before any code:
1. How a command that can spend fits the kernel's risk and approval policy.
2. How write permission on a platform is opened, given that #2275 refuses any token that holds `ads_management`.
3. How the platform calls are made exactly once, and what happens when one is lost.
4. What personal data targeting and audiences involve.
5. In what order the slices are built so that nothing spends before an approved plan.

## Decision drivers

- **Nothing spends without a human approving exactly that plan, within a cap the owner set.**
- **Read-only stays the safe default**: an org that never asks to run ads is unaffected, and its refusal of write scopes holds.
- One write path, tenancy, sealed credentials and the job egress invariant are unchanged (AGENTS.md rules).
- OPC and SME first: the owner's own accounts must work without waiting for a platform review.
- No personal data beyond what a person can already see in the platform's own targeting options.

## Decision (proposed)

### 1. Spend is R3, and goes through a proposal

A command that can start or raise spend (launch, activate or resume, raise a budget, extend a schedule, set or raise a cap, enable writing) declares `sideEffect: 'financial'` and is **R3**: a proposal, an approver who is not an author of it (P10), a fresh second factor (P11), the approver's own permission and legal entity (P12), escalation above the org's money threshold (P08), executed once (P13). What only reduces or stops spend (pause, lower a budget, shorten, archive) is R2 `external`; drafting is R1. The approval **names the largest amount the plan can spend** and binds the input hash of exactly that plan. No new row of the derived-risk table is needed: the existing rule (`financial` is R3) already does it, and the registry test keeps each command at or above it.

**Who asks.** Only an owner can ask to open spend: the org switch, an account, a cap set or raised, and launches opened again after a block. An admin or a member cannot ask, and an owner or an admin approves (Q2), never the one who asked (P10), except on the one-person path below. Without this, two admins could open spend between themselves with no owner involved, against points 2, 3 and 7a (the preparers of W0a-2 are the owner's, #2294).

**The one-person-company path (P10).** An org whose only eligible human is its owner has nobody to be the second pair of eyes, so P10 lets that owner approve their own plan, and the audit row says `self_approved`. Self-approval relaxes nothing else: P11 still needs a fresh second factor, the approval card still names the largest amount, and the caps of point 2 still bind. An owner with no verified second factor cannot switch writing on (point 3) until they enrol one, so a one-person org never reaches R3 without a factor. The moment a second eligible human joins, P10 applies as for any org and the owner can no longer approve a plan they wrote.

### 2. Per-account and per-org spend caps; none set, no launch

The owner sets a daily and a monthly cap per ad account and per org (R3, owner only, step-up). A launch is refused until caps exist, and again when the committed spend (live and scheduled budgets plus the new plan) would pass one, at approval and at execution. The platform's own spending limit is read and shown; Ludy's caps are a second fence.

- **The execution check is the authoritative one, and it runs under a lock.** The approval-time check only warns early. At execution the launch step takes a per-org transaction advisory lock, recomputes the committed spend from `crove_ads` rows, and in the same transaction writes the plan's commitment before any platform call; the lock is released at commit. Two launches approved at the same moment therefore serialise, and the second sees the first's commitment and is refused if together they would pass a cap. A commitment is released only when its objects are archived or their schedule has ended. A test runs launches in parallel and asserts that the committed spend never passes a cap.
- **As built in W2-2 (#2296): the launch ledger, and where the lock is.** The commitment is a row of `crove_ads.ad_launch`, made by the execution of the approved proposal of a plan and by nothing else (the database's guard `crove_ads.launch_guard` holds the insert to that proposal, its draft, its hash, its budget and days and its largest amount, while the switches are on), one row per draft. It commits the plan's daily budget on every day from its first day to `commit_until`, which starts at the end of the flight and can only move earlier (a launch that is stopped keeps what ran; one that never started, cancelled or failed before activation, commits nothing). The check is the org's lock the approval already takes (`lockedSetting`, a transaction advisory lock): under it the command reads every launch that still commits, in every account of the org whatever the approver's legal entity scope (a definer, `crove_ads.launch_commitments`, for owners, admins and the system), judges the plan against every cap in force (a daily cap or a monthly cap alone is enough; the account's caps count the account's launches, an org cap every launch of the org, in the cap's currency), and writes the row in the same transaction, so two launches approved together queue on the lock and the second sees the first. The request for approval runs the same check as an early warning. Cancelling a queued launch (`ads.launch.cancel`, an owner's or an admin's) and the stops (`ads.write.disable`, `ads.account.disable_write`, `ads.pause_all`) release the launches that have not started; the state columns move only along the graph the guard holds, by the actor that may make each move (the writer's moves are the system's, W2-3). Nothing launches in W2-2: no queue, no job and no platform call, so a queued row only holds its amount; the caps are read again before the writer's first platform call (W2-3), and a cap lowered after the approval does not release a commitment. Two things W2-3 and W2-4 must keep: a draft approved before this table existed (W1) has no ledger row, since the guard allows an insert only inside the approval itself, so the writer launches only a draft that has one; and the ledger goes with its account (the deletion of an account's history and the org's purge delete it by cascade), so a launch that has started must be wound down before its account's history is deleted.
- **Caps have a currency.** A cap is a decimal string in the currency's own units (dong, or dollars with cents, at most two decimals) with an ISO currency code (money rules of AGENTS.md; never a JS number); a platform's minor-unit budgets are converted with decimal.js before they are compared. Each cap carries its own currency. An account cap is in the account's currency. An org cap is in any ISO currency (VND unless the owner sets another). A launch is refused with `ADS_CAP_CURRENCY` unless every org cap that applies is in the account's currency: converting between currencies would add a rate the approver did not see, so it is a later decision with its own amendment.

### 3. Write permission is a separate, explicit, conditional step

- Three layers: the deployment has the platform app (a separate app for writing is recommended); the **org** switches ad writing on (owner, step-up, audited, default off); each **ad account** is enabled for writing (default off) through a **new connection step** that goes through the platform's dialog again.
- **#2275's guard is not removed but made conditional**: with the org switch off the connector refuses any token that holds `ads_management` or `business_management`; with it on, the connector asks for and accepts them and records the full set on the connection. Read-only orgs keep refusing write scopes, with the tests of #2275 plus the switch-on path.
- The Ads screen says per account whether its token can only read or can also run ads.
- **The write token is its own credential.**
  - *Sealed separately.* It is a second sealed credential of the connection (ADR-0025), apart from the read token, opened by the worker alone and only inside a write job. The API and the read jobs never hold it, so a leak of the read path cannot spend.
  - *Require App Secret.* Every write call carries `appsecret_proof`, and the write app has "Require App Secret" switched on at Meta, so a token copied out of Ludy is useless without the app secret.
  - *Revoked at Meta on disconnect or switch-off.* Disconnecting an account or turning the org switch off makes the worker revoke the write token at Meta (the platform's call that removes the app's permissions, the one call of the writer that is not about an ad object) and then delete the sealed copy. A revoke that fails is retried and shown on the Ads screen as not yet revoked; the sealed copy stays until it succeeds.
  - *Queued writes stop when the switch goes off.* The write job checks the org switch and the account's write flag again before each step, not only when it was queued. Turning the switch off cancels every launch that has not started and every step not yet run; a step already in flight finishes and is recorded; objects already created stay paused. Nothing queued can spend after the owner switched writing off.

### 4. A second adapter interface for writing, in separate files

`AdWriter` in `@crove/ad-platform`, one file per platform, offered only where the org switch is on: create campaign, ad set, creative and ad, set status, budget and schedule, read an object's state, and revoke the write token (point 3). **No delete of any ad object** (archive is a status). A test pins the endpoints each writer calls, its host binding and the absence of any other method. The reader interface and its tests are unchanged, so no reader file can acquire a write.

**What W0b does and defers (#2294).** The write connection of W0b is a second channel connection of the same platform account (provider `metawrite_ads`, `sandboxwrite_ads` for the offline sandbox) with its own sealed credential, attached to the ad account the read connection linked, made by the owner while the org switch is on. W0b has no writer that calls the platform, no write queue and no job, so **the revoke at the platform of point 3 cannot run yet**: closing a write connection (switching writing off, withdrawing an account, disconnecting it, removing it, the platform's deletion request) drops the sealed token from Ludy at once, and the platform's copy stays valid until it expires or the person removes the app in the platform's settings. The Ads screen and the runbook say so plainly. W2 adds the revoke (`DELETE /me/permissions`) for both kinds of token, the one a write connection holds and any read connection that holds spend scopes, and **W2 cannot merge without it**. The conditional guard of #2275 and the closing of the read connections that hold spend scopes come together, in W0b-2 (built there): while the org switch is off the read connector refuses `ads_management` and `business_management`; while it is on it accepts and records them, and switching writing off then closes every connection that holds a spend-capable token and drops its sealed token, so none stays in Ludy after a switch-off. Which connections those are is not told by the recorded scopes alone: a Meta permission belongs to the pair of the app and the person, so with the one app that serves reading and writing today, once the owner grants `ads_management` every read token of that person for the app can change campaigns, though its recorded scopes still say `ads_read`. Who that person is cannot be told for sure afterwards (a second owner replaced them on the account, the account was removed, Meta did not name them, the connection was never linked), so the close fails safe: besides every connection whose recorded scopes hold either permission, it closes every Meta reading connection of the org once running ads was on or the org has made a Meta write connection at any time (the closed write connection's row stays after its account is gone, so the evidence does not go with the account; and a consent at Meta may never have reached Ludy, a switch-off between the dialog and its callback for one, so a switch that was on is evidence enough). The write app may be the read ads app (the owner's own account: one classic app serves both) or an app of its own, as the owner's answer Q7 asks for before Ludy Cloud serves other businesses (#2366): Meta's data deletion callback verifies a request against every secret it holds, the read Ads app's, the write app's and the Pages app's, and queues it under the hash keyed with the secret that verified it, so a person's write grant is found whichever app they removed (user ids are app-scoped, so the two hashes of one person differ). A deployment others reach needs a write app that is neither the read ads app nor the Pages app, and the config refuses the read or Pages app there: with the one app a person's reading tokens can change campaigns in every org of the deployment while the switch-off closes them one org at a time, and with an app of its own no reading token can hold the permission. The screen then asks the owner to reconnect read-only, and a read-only reconnect after a switch-off works only once the owner has removed the app in Facebook settings (Business integrations) or the scopes were revoked there. W2's revoke removes the permission for the pair (the permission-revoke call of the Graph API for the pair), not only one token. As built in W0b-2: the sign-in passes the switch's state to the reading connector (`ExchangeInput.spendScopesAllowed`), only for an owner (an admin who edits the dialog to ask for more must not seal a token that can spend), and the account link refuses a connection whose recorded scopes hold a spend permission while the switch is off, closing it in its own transaction and saying so in its answer (a throw would roll the closing back, and a connection reconnected with a new token is the same row; the setting is read `FOR SHARE`, so a switch-off in flight cannot leave one linked); `ads.write.disable` closes, with `kernel.channel.disconnect` and the status reason `spend_scopes`, the connections above; the Ads screen shows the reason and asks for a read-only reconnect. The cost of failing safe is one reconnect for every Meta reading connection at each switch-off, and the person who granted the permission removing the app in Facebook first (which also removes the other permissions they gave the app, such as Pages); a durable set of grantors would narrow it, and a write app of its own makes it unnecessary, since the reading app then never holds the permission: the API passes `sharedApp` to `ads.write.disable`, true when the config has the write app as the read ads app or the Pages app (`metaWriteAppShared`), false with an app of its own, where only the recorded scopes close; left out, the close fails safe. The config decides at the time of the switch-off, so a self-hosted deployment that moves from a shared write app to an app of its own, or drops the write app, switches running ads off once before the change (or reconnects its Meta reading connections): reading tokens made while the app was shared may still hold the permission, and the narrowed close no longer sees them. **Not covered, so the config keeps refusing the write app on a public deployment:** the permission is the person's for the app across every org of the deployment, but the close is per org. A person who granted it in one org and has reading tokens in another org that never switched running ads on keeps spend-capable tokens there. The refusal stays until a separate write app exists (#2366); a self-hosted instance with several orgs and one person in them has the same gap and is told so in the runbook.

**Carried to W2 from the independent review of W0b-1.** The owner's role is checked when a write connection is attached; a demoted or removed owner's connection stays open, so W2's write job requires its connector to be an owner still, or a change of role closes their write connections. The switch-off closes each write connection in its own nested command inside one transaction, so one failing close fails the switch-off and leaves writing on: W2's revoke at the platform runs after the commit, from the queue, never inside the switch-off. A write connection is never marked expired and `AdWriter.verify` is not called yet, so "can run ads" can outlive the token; W2 reads the permission before the first launch. The guard and the switch-off close are tested in `ads.db.test.ts`, as W0a's switch guard is; moving them under `test/invariant` is a separate two-step change.

**The revoke and the pause need a sealed copy (owner decision 2026-11-03, #2296; [ADR-0025](0025-channel-layer.md) amendment).** W0b drops a closed connection's sealed token at once, which leaves nothing to revoke or pause with. W2 therefore keeps, in the definer-only table `crove_ads.ad_token_seal`, a sealed copy of the token of every closed write connection (and of every closed read connection whose recorded scopes hold a spend permission, whatever the reason it is closed for), made in the closing transaction, for **at most seven days**: the worker opens it for `DELETE /me/permissions` and, from W2-4, the one call that pauses an object, and nothing else (a narrowed view of the writer, a test on it, and no command or query ever reads it). **The owner approved this for Meta's ads providers (`metawrite_ads`, and `meta_ads` with a recorded spend permission) and the offline sandbox only; another platform needs its own decision.** **The copy opens after the member who connected it has left** the org or stopped being an owner or admin, which relaxes the membership rule of `credential_get` for these calls alone and within the seven days: ending the access is its only use, and a member's removal must not leave the permission valid at the platform. Two tables: `crove_ads.ad_token_seal` (key and envelope; no grant to any role) and `crove_ads.ad_token_revoke` (the state: the connection, its provider and account, the kind and reason, the grantor's keyed hash, the count, the next try and how it ended; tenant roles read it, only definers write it and none deletes). **As built in W2-1:** the copy is made by a trigger on the credential table, in the statement that drops the credential, so no path (the generic disconnect included) can skip it and no live credential is copied; a worker-only definer opens it, in the org's system context, for an obligation the sweep has claimed, and counts the try as it opens. It is wiped when the revoke worked, **when the person had already removed the app (Meta error 190 with subcode 458; every other refusal of the token, such as 463, 467 or 460, means the grant may still stand and ends as "revoke by hand", never as revoked)**, after ten attempts or seven days (the screen then says to revoke by hand), at once when nothing can be revoked from it, **when another live connection of the same grantor in any org needs the grant (the revoke is the person's, for the app: a boolean-only definer says so before the call, and the obligation ends as `shared_grant`)**, when the account is connected again (the new connection's credential cancels the pending revoke), by the org's purge, and after a member's removal only once the revoke was tried (the sweep closes the write connection of a member who left, which holds and revokes it). A switch-off therefore runs, from the queue and after the commit: pause what was launched, then revoke, then wipe. An ads read connection closed by the fail-safe alone (no spend permission recorded) is closed locally and not revoked at Meta: the grantor's write token, when it exists, removes the permission for the pair, and otherwise the screen keeps telling the owner to remove the app in Facebook settings.

### 5. Exactly once, objects paused first, reconcile before retry

Each platform call is an `external_call` step with a deterministic key (`ads.launch:<launch id>:<step>`). Objects are created **paused** and activated last, so a launch that stops half way has spent nothing. Every created object carries a tag in its name, because Meta offers no idempotency key for creation [U]; an `ambiguous` step starts a reconcile job that lists the account's objects by that tag before anything is retried. A rejected ad is shown with the platform's reason and never re-submitted automatically.

**As built in W2-3 (2026-11-03, #2296): the writer and the launch job, and nothing un-pauses.** A queued launch (W2-2) is run by the job of the queue `ads-launch` (ids only; the approval writes the event `ads.launch.queued` and a ten-minute sweep writes it again for a launch that waited), which creates the plan's campaign, ad set and, for each text variant, a creative and an ad at the platform, **every one paused**, and records each platform id in `crove_ads.ad_object`. A launch **ends W2-3 as "objects created, paused"**: state `launching`, step `paused`, its commitment kept against the caps until W2-4 activates it. The decisions of this slice, each by the Marketing PO and the Platform PO's review:
- **The writer is a frozen view.** The job is given `LaunchWriter` (`@crove/ad-platform`): the four creates, `findByTag`, `resolveTargeting` and `verify`, built by one function from a writer as a frozen object of closures, so it cannot revoke a token, change a status or delete anything, and a source test pins the field `channels.adWriters` to it. `MetaAdWriter` (its own file) names an endpoint list a test pins: one `POST act_<id>/<edge>` call site for the four creates, which sets `status=PAUSED` last so no input can put another status in (a creative has no status at Meta to set; it does not deliver until an ad that uses it is active), `GET` of an edge by the tag, `GET search`, and `POST <object id>` with `PAUSED` or `ARCHIVED` and no other value. No source has the word `ACTIVE` and no source outside the package calls a status change: W2-4 adds the activation behind its own approval and stop path and the same caps re-check.
- **Budgets are exact.** The ledger's daily budget (a decimal string in the account currency's own units) is converted to Meta's minor unit with Meta's offset per currency by string arithmetic, never a number: VND, JPY and KRW as they are, USD, EUR, GBP, AUD, CAD, SGD and THB times a hundred [U: the table is written from Meta's documentation and the live check confirms it]. A currency with no verified offset stops the launch (`currency_unsupported`); a fraction the offset cannot carry is refused, never rounded. A hundred-fold error would spend real money, so it is tested with a recording fetch for VND and USD and as a property over many amounts, and before the first create the writer reads the account's own currency from Meta and refuses an account whose currency is not the one the plan was approved in (`CURRENCY_MISMATCH`: the last synced currency could be stale).
- **A Page for every ad.** Meta runs every ad as a Page. A plan with no Page connection stops the launch as `failed` (`page_required`) before any call; a Page that is no longer active stops it as `needs_attention` (`page_inactive`).
- **Objectives live at Meta.** Meta's writer has the parameters of Traffic only; Engagement and Messenger on a Meta account stop as `failed` (`objective_unsupported`) until the live check has passed for them. The sandbox runs all three.
- **Targeting is resolved by the platform.** The plan stores names; before the ad set the job resolves each region and interest with Meta's targeting search (a read) and a name that does not resolve to exactly one id stops the launch as `needs_attention` (`targeting_unknown`) with the names listed in the audit row, nothing spent.
- **Media files are not uploaded.** A plan with a media-library file stops on Meta as `failed` (`media_unsupported`); the sandbox takes it. Media upload is a later slice.
- **What ends a launch before any call is `failed`, and releases the commitment** (a frozen plan cannot be repaired by a retry): no Page, an objective or a currency the writer has no table for, a media file, a plan that is no longer the one approved (its hash). **What the environment does is `needs_attention`, with its code, and keeps the commitment**: a refused token, a token that cannot run ads, a refusal of the platform for a call, a name the platform does not know. A cap lowered since the approval cancels a queued launch before its first call (`cap_lowered`).
- **Exactly once.** Every call is a row of the kernel's `external_call` ledger, `ads.launch:<launch>:<attempt>:<step>`, inserted `started` and committed before the platform is called (`ads.launch.begin`), and settled afterwards (`ads.launch.record`). A row begin finds again is never called a second time blind: once it is old enough to be lost (five minutes) or marked unclear (a minute) the job asks the platform for the objects that carry the launch's tag. An object is matched by the **tail of its name**, the tag and what the object is (` set`, ` creative 1`, ` ad 2`: ASCII, so nothing a platform does to the plan's own text in a name, a trim or a re-encoding, can change it); every name is cut to 100 characters with the tail kept. Exactly one object with this step's tail is the one and is recorded; none at all is missing and the call is made once more (the row counts one call more, and only the run that took the reconcile may record `missing`, so two jobs at the same moment never both make it); two with the tail, or one with the tag and a tail that is not a sibling's, cannot be told apart from it, so **nothing is created and the launch needs attention** (`reconcile_unclear`). A step that stays unclear (an answer that cannot be told, a platform that cannot be asked) is not asked about for ever: after three calls or an hour the launch needs attention as well. A worker that dies after the platform answered and before the record leaves a `started` row that the next run reconciles. **An object the platform made is never dropped**: if a concurrent run stopped the launch while the call was in flight, the record still tracks the object (and settles the call) and leaves the launch as it is; a result that cannot be recorded leaves an audit row with the step and the platform's id.
- **The record is a record.** `ad_object.configured_status` says what Ludy set (paused, or archived) and is never an input: no job, command or writer reads it to decide a platform call (a source test pins it), the database lets only the system write it (a person's write is refused by `crove_ads.object_guard`, a column check allows nothing but paused and archived) and a status change at the platform goes only through a command with its own approval or stop path.
- **A person's stop must not release spend that ran or is in flight** (the acceptance line of W2-3). `crove_ads.launch_guard` holds it: an owner or an admin stops a launch that was started (launching, needs attention or live) only with its commitment held to at least the earlier of what it was and today in the account's own time zone, and never cleared; only the system, which knows what the writer did, releases it. When no zone is recorded, or the database does not know the name, today is taken as the latest day any zone can be on (tomorrow in UTC), so a person can release the least.
- **The switch.** The worker builds Meta's writer only when `ADS_LIVE_LAUNCH` is on (off by default: a Meta launch stays queued, untouched), and the server refuses it on a public `PUBLIC_URL` until W2-4 (the activation, the pause on switch-off) and W2-5 (the live-check runbook) are built; turning it on in production is an owner-approved step list.
- **Tokens.** The write token is opened by the credential broker inside the job, once per step, from the write connection's own sealed copy through the kernel's `kernel.channel.credential` query (which refuses a connection that is no longer active or whose connector may no longer use it); it reaches the writer's calls and nothing else, and never a log line, an error, an `external_call` row, an audit row or a command's input (redaction tests, a source test).

### 6. Personal data: none in targeting, audiences out of the first slices

Targeting by location, age, gender, language, interests and placement stores parameters, not people. **Custom audiences (customer lists), lookalikes, pixel and Conversions API events, and lead forms are personal data of third parties and are out of these slices**; they need a separate amendment and a lawyer's reading under the Law on Personal Data Protection first. Special ad categories are declared as none, and an account the owner marks as running one is refused until a lawyer has read the rules for Vietnam.

### 7. Agents draft, humans launch

An agent may write **drafts** (R1, reaching a human as a proposal); it never launches, activates, resumes, raises, extends, sets a cap or enables writing. This amends the write allowlist of ADR-0029 for the draft commands only; the test that enumerates tables from the catalog and refuses agent writes keeps every other `crove_ads` table closed.

### 7a. A fast kill switch: pause everything Ludy launched

`ads.pause_all` is an **R2** `external` command (owner, admin and marketing members; no proposal and no second factor, because it only stops spend and speed is the point). It pauses every campaign and ad set that Ludy launched in the org, found from the recorded object ids and checked against the `ludy:` name tag, as `external_call` steps with deterministic keys (`ads.pause_all:<run id>:<object>`) so a double click pauses once. It also sets an org flag `launches_blocked`; while the flag is set every launch, activation and resume is refused, and only an owner clears it (R3, step-up). Turning the org switch off runs the same pause first. The screen offers it as one clearly marked button, and it is audited with the number of objects paused and any the platform refused, each with its reason.

### 8. Ads still never posts to the books

Spend reaches Kế toán through intake and the platform invoices (slice D). Ads shows the committed spend beside the actual one and posts nothing.

### 9. Slices

W0 switch, access, caps, kill switch; W1 draft and validate (nothing leaves Ludy); W2 launch on Meta; W3 boost a published post; W4 manage; W5 ads on the Social calendar; W6 Automated Boost, and Google writing in its own ADR. ## Decisions for the owner

## Consequences

- Owners can build, approve and launch a Meta campaign from Ludy, see Meta's review state, and change or stop it, with a record of who approved which amount.
- New protected surface in every write slice: migrations, new commands that move money, a new job queue and adapter, OAuth scope changes, new RLS matrix rows. Each PR gets the independent security review and the two-step invariant route.
- Ludy Cloud cannot run other businesses' ads on Meta before Advanced access, App Review and Business Verification (owner steps); the owner's own accounts need none.
- A write adapter is code that can spend a customer's money on a platform: its tests pin its endpoints, and a platform version or rule change is a spending risk, so each writer pins one version with recorded-answer tests and a live check before it is used.
- Meta's review and rate limits shape the user's wait; the screen says so rather than hiding it.

## Alternatives considered

- **One adapter interface for read and write.** Rejected: an interface that can write makes every reader a potential spender and weakens the test that reader files call no write endpoint.
- **Upgrade a read-only connection to write in place.** Rejected: a token's scope would change without the person seeing a dialog; a new explicit connection step is auditable.
- **Remove #2275's guard.** Rejected: read-only orgs should keep refusing write scopes; the guard becomes conditional on the org switch.
- **Activate objects as they are created.** Rejected: a half-finished launch would spend; paused creation with activation last costs one more call.
- **Rely on the platform's own spending limit instead of Ludy's caps.** Rejected as the only fence: it is not Ludy's to set, may be absent, and does not know the plan the approver signed; read and shown beside the caps.
- **A default cap.** Offered as Q3 option B; not recommended: a high default is a surprise bill.
- **Let an agent launch inside a cap.** Rejected for the first slices: an R3 action by a non-user actor is a review (P05) and the owner asked for approval before spend; revisit with Automated Boost (Q8).
- **Customer-list audiences in the first slices.** Rejected: personal data transfer needing a legal basis and a lawyer first.

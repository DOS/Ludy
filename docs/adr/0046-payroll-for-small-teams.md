## Addendum 2026-11-04: payroll is its own app

The owner decided on 2026-11-04 in the Accounting PO chat that payroll is not part of Kế toán: "Nếu Tính lương thì sẽ nằm Riêng" (if it is payroll, it sits apart). This follows the all-in-one suites Ludy measures itself against (Odoo, MISA AMIS; `docs/product/scope.md` section 6), which give HR and payroll an app of their own that posts its totals to accounting. It changes these points of the decision and nothing else:

- **Its own module and app.** Employees, employments, dependents, the rule-driven calculator's runs and payslips live in a new module `hr` (schema `crove_hr`) that contributes its own app to the shell (ADR-0031), working name Ludy Nhân sự (English: Ludy HR); the owner may rename it. The rule data stays in `@crove/l10n-vn` (point 2).
- **Its own permissions.** Point 4's `finance.payroll.read` and `finance.payroll.run` become `hr.payroll.read` and `hr.payroll.run`, with the same defaults (owners and admins only) and the same `sensitive` queries. A finance role alone reads no payroll line.
- **Posting through finance's proposal path.** The `hr` module depends on `finance`. Approving a month in the HR app creates the posting proposal (point 6) in finance through finance's own command; Kế toán sees and approves that proposal with its totals by account, like any other, and never the lines behind it. No foreign key crosses from `crove_hr` to `crove_finance`; the proposal carries the run's id as its source.
- **The coworker.** The HR app gets its own coworker once the owner names one (DESIGN.md section 1.1); until then the HR screens name the general manager (`{gm}`), and the AI features keep their own `AI_FEATURES` keys under the meter (point 5).

The builds #2529 to #2534 and #2539 to #2541 build into the `hr` module; their order and slicing in the plan stay.

## Context and problem statement

A small business with staff runs payroll every month: gross pay, the employee's and the employer's social, health and unemployment insurance, personal income tax withholding with family deductions, a payslip, the posting (334, 338x, 3335 and the expense accounts), the payments, and then two declarations, one to the social insurance agency and one to the tax authority. Ludy has none of this.

The survey of the plan (section 3) found:

- **The rules moved in 2026 and are spread over many texts.** The Personal Income Tax Law 109/2025 (five brackets, personal deduction 15.5 million, dependent 6.2 million) applies to the whole 2026 tax period; salary tax is declared quarterly; the Social Insurance Law 2024 ties the contribution floor and cap to a reference level that rose from 2,340,000 to 2,530,000 on 2026-07-01 (so months before and after July differ, and sources still print the old cap); Decision 366/QĐ-BHXH changed the insurance forms; the regional minimum wage changes again on 2027-01-01 (Decree 399/2026, published the day of the survey). Only the Social Insurance Law (consolidated), the Labor Code 2019 and Circular 152 were read in primary text; the rest rests on commentary, and some figures conflict between sources (the withholding rules for January to June, the deduction for health and education, the household-owner start date).
- **There is no public API** for the insurance agency or the tax authority for a software vendor. Employers file through the agency's portal or an intermediary (I-VAN), and through the tax portal with XML from the tax authority's own tool; the official XSD is not published.
- **Employee records are the most sensitive data an org holds.** Identity numbers, bank accounts, dependents and salaries; the Personal Data Protection Law 91/2025 (in force 2026-01-01) has an article on employee data, and the books (the payroll) must be kept longer than the person may want.
- **Charts differ by regime**: the unemployment insurance account is 3385 under Circular 133 and 3386 under Circular 99 (both already in Ludy's charts), and the form numbers of the payroll vouchers differ (02-LĐTL is the payroll sheet under TT133 and the bonus sheet under TT99).

## Decision

1. **Payroll computes by code, from rule data, and a person approves.** The calculator is pure domain code in `@crove/l10n-vn` (decimal arithmetic, no JS numbers for money): insurance by fund for employee and employer, the contribution base with its floor and ceiling, the personal income tax by the method the contract calls for, the checks of the Labor Code that bite (the 30% cap on deductions, the minimum wage, overtime rates). A model never states a number that enters the pay sheet.
2. **Rules are dated data with a source and a status, not constants.** One table per kind (regional minimum wage, insurance rates, the reference level behind the floor and cap, the PIT brackets and deductions, the exempt allowances), each row with an effective date, the text it comes from, and a status `confirmed` or `commentary`. A run takes the rows in force at its pay month. **A run cannot be approved while any rule it used is `commentary`**; the accountant flips a row by a pull request once checked against the primary text. A rule change is a new dated row, never an edit of an old one, so a closed month recomputes to the same figures.
3. **An employee is not an org member.** Employees (per legal entity) and their employment records (contract type, dates, probation, pay components, workplace region, dependents, payment account) are their own tables. A run is a header and lines for one month and one legal entity; once approved it is immutable: a correction is an adjustment line in a later run, or a reversal of the posting under the period lock.
4. **Sensitive by default, narrow by role.** Payroll reads and writes need new permissions (`finance.payroll.read`, `finance.payroll.run`), default to owners and admins only, and every payroll query is `sensitive` (the audit row is committed before the read, plan section 5.2). Columns are classified: the identity number `national_id`, the bank account `payment`, names, addresses and dependents' names `pii`, salaries `sensitive` (the survey could not show that the law calls salary sensitive; the class is chosen to restrict access and audit, not as a legal conclusion). **No image of an identity document is ever stored** (the law names such images sensitive and nothing in the payroll needs one). The identity number and the bank account are stored under the employee's per-subject key (ADR-0017), so an erasure after the retention period shreds the key and leaves the append-only run lines readable only as amounts. The personal-data registry lists every payroll column with its handling: payroll lines `keep_legal` until the retention the lawyer confirms, the rest `erase`.
5. **The model sees no names and no identifiers.** For the coworker's help (preparing a month's sheet, flagging, explaining a payslip) the sheet is pseudonymized before it reaches the model: an alias per employee, amounts and component labels only, never a name, identity number, tax code, bank account or address. The model's output is checked in code (every figure it states must be in what it was given) and mapped back by the alias. Model calls go through `metered()` under new `AI_FEATURES` keys; the core works with AI off.
6. **One posting proposal per run, accounts by the entity's regime.** The run proposes one entry through the finance proposal path: the accrual of gross pay by function (the employee's function picks the expense account of the entity's chart), the employer's insurance and union fee, the employee's deductions, the PIT withheld. A map from component to account per regime (`tt133`, `tt99`) holds the differences (3385 or 3386 for unemployment insurance), never a constant in code. The payment of net pay and the remittances to the agency and the tax authority are vouchers that bank matching clears like any other.
7. **Ludy exports, it does not file and does not move money.** It produces the monthly list for the insurance agency (the D02-LT layout of Decision 366), the quarterly and annual PIT declaration data and schedules, the withholding certificate data, and a bank bulk-salary file per configurable template, as spreadsheets; XML for the tax authority's tool follows only once a lawyer has cleared the use of the XSD copied from its installer. The person files and signs; a payment is made in the person's own bank. A cash payment of 5 million VND or more is flagged because it makes the pay non-deductible for corporate income tax.
8. **What it does not model**: time-keeping, leave balances, an employee portal, piece rates, foreign workers (the non-resident rate and the work permit), severance and job-loss calculation, the 13th month, annual finalization by authorization, benefit claims. The first slice takes hours of overtime and unpaid days as monthly inputs typed or imported from a spreadsheet.

## Consequences

- New tenant tables (employee, employment, dependent, payroll run, run line) each need the two-step rule (an invariant-only PR first), `org_purge`, manifest entries with data classes, permissions with their default roles, and entries in the personal-data registry. The migrations are protected paths: the Platform PO reviews each.
- Rule data in `@crove/l10n-vn` is released with the code, so a self-hoster gets a rule change by upgrading; a rule table in the database would let an org change it without a release but would also let a wrong edit change a closed month. The plan keeps rules in code with dated rows.
- The accountant's check becomes a gate with a visible mechanism (a row's status), not a sentence in a plan.
- A payslip is data for a person to hand to an employee; sending it by email is a later, separately approved step because the message holds a salary.
- Employee data in Ludy Cloud depends on a legal answer (the processor's exemption from the impact assessment, cross-border storage); until then the plan builds for a self-hosted install and a cloud install with the rules the lawyer confirms.

## Alternatives considered

- **Employees as org members.** Rejected: most employees never log in, and a member can read what a role allows; payroll data needs its own permission and audit.
- **Rules as constants or as an editable table.** Rejected: constants hide a change and a table lets a wrong edit move a closed month; dated rows with a gate do neither.
- **Letting the model compute or explain from the named sheet.** Rejected: the survey could not say which employee data the law calls sensitive, and a model gains nothing from a name.
- **Filing through an intermediary or the agency's portal from Ludy.** Rejected: no public API was found; it would need the org's digital signature (a credential an agent must not hold) and an intermediary contract.
- **Storing an identity document image for the register.** Rejected: nothing in the payroll needs one.

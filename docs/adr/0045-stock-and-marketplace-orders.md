## Context and problem statement

Ludy has goods codes for the MISA export (#1398) and purchase lines that carry a description, a unit, a quantity and a price, but no stock: no item, no balance, no cost of goods sold. A small seller needs on-hand quantities, the cost of what it sold (accounts 156 and 632) and, for a household business on the income method, the goods book S2d-HKD of Circular 152/2025.

Most small sellers in Vietnam sell on marketplaces. A marketplace order is not an invoice: it has no invoice number and no buyer in the seller's books, there are hundreds a month, the platform pays the seller in batches after deducting fees (and, for household sellers, the VAT and personal income tax it withholds under Decree 117/2025), and the platform's own fee invoices arrive separately. The survey of the plan (section 5) found that the three marketplaces do offer APIs, but that a small seller can connect its own app on only some of them without an approval (Lazada: yes, by a personal self-developed account; TikTok Shop: yes, once the shop has an account manager; Shopee Vietnam: probably only Yêu Thích and Mall shops), that a shared app for many sellers means a partner registration and a security review, that every platform masks buyer data and requires deleting it within about 90 days, and that the report files a seller can download have first-party help pages for only part of their layout (Shopee's income workbook by sheet and column group, TikTok Shop's order export by header) while the Vietnamese headers of the rest, the payout reference of every platform and the bank text of a payout were not found.

The decisions below fix how stock, cost, marketplace orders and the household client kind fit the books that exist.

## Decision

1. **Stock is a ledger of moves, not a balance.** An item belongs to a legal entity (code, name, unit, kind, trade, the stock account it is booked to). Every change of quantity is an append-only `stock_move` (signed quantity, date, the source: a purchase line, a sale line, a marketplace order line, a count, an adjustment). On hand is the sum of moves, never a column that is updated. A move is never edited; a mistake is a reversing move. One warehouse is implicit in the first slices; a warehouse column with a default arrives with the step that needs it. Lots, expiry and manufacturing wait (plan section 9).
2. **Cost is the weighted average per item per calendar month, fixed when the month is closed.** Unit cost of goods out = (opening value + value in during the month) / (opening quantity + quantity in during the month). This is the formula Circular 152/2025 gives for the goods book in its primary text, and weighted average is allowed for enterprises under Circular 133/2016 and Circular 99/2025 (commentary; the accountant confirms, #61). Between closes the screens show a provisional running cost, labelled so. Moving average and FIFO are settings of a later step, per legal entity, never changed within a closed year.
3. **The cost of goods sold is one proposal per month and entity.** Closing a month computes each item's average by code and proposes one entry (Dr 632, Cr 156, plus Dr 156 / Cr 632 for returns) through the finance proposal path; a person approves it. It is deterministic: a model never states a quantity or an amount. A closed month is changed only by a reversal under the period-lock rules.
4. **Stock comes in from approved purchase lines and goes out from approved sale lines and marketplace order lines.** A purchase line on a goods account becomes a stock-in at its amount (plus VAT the entity cannot deduct, when its rule says so); a sale line becomes a stock-out. A line is matched to an item by a deterministic chain: an item code or a marketplace SKU alias; else an alias the org's people taught by correcting (as #1399 does for accounts); else a suggestion from the finance coworker, shown for a person to confirm. An unmatched line stays in a list ("Chưa gán hàng") and moves nothing.
5. **A marketplace order is not an invoice and is not a sales document.** It is a `channel_order` with lines (platform, the platform's order number, dates, status, amounts as decimal strings, SKU) in finance, never a row of the purchase and sales documents. **No buyer personal data is stored**: not a name, phone, address or email; the importer reads columns from an allow-list per platform profile and drops the rest. Stock goes out when the order is shipped and comes back when a return is received (the point is a setting with that default, the accountant confirms the revenue recognition point).
6. **The platform is a party, its payouts clear its balance.** Importing a platform's income or payout report posts, per settlement period, Dr 131 (the platform) and Cr 511 for the sales it paid for, net of the seller's own discounts, and records the platform's fee and withheld-tax lines in the settlement record. The payout bank line is matched (the bank matching of #2385) against the platform's open balance and posts Dr 112 for the money received, Dr 331 (the platform) for fees already invoiced or Dr 1388 for fees whose invoice has not arrived, Dr 333 for tax the platform withheld and settled for the seller, Cr 131. The platform's own fee e-invoice then enters as a purchase document as today and clears 1388. The exact accounts and the household case are for the accountant to confirm before build 5 merges.
7. **Files first, the platform's API second.** The first way in is the report files a seller downloads by hand from the seller portal, read in the parsing sandbox, **staged**, and stored only when a member confirms, exactly as bank statement files are (#2142: the file's sender never chooses the account, a forged file stores nothing). Columns are mapped by header detection per platform profile, a header the profile does not know is proposed by the finance coworker and confirmed by a person, and the confirmed mapping is learned per org and platform. An API is a `marketplace` connection kind of the "Tích hợp" section (ADR-0044 point 2), with the seller's own credentials sealed (ADR-0025), a port with an offline provider, polling with push as a hint, off unless the deployment enables the provider. **A real adapter is built only for a platform where a seller can connect itself, or after the owner has registered Ludy as a partner and the seller's data terms are met.** That registration is an owner decision, not a build step (plan section 10).
8. **Uploaded exports hold personal data the rows do not.** The original file is kept until the staged read is confirmed or rejected and is then deleted after 90 days; the rows keep the file's hash and row number as provenance. If the accountant says the original must be kept as evidence, the file is kept and access to it narrowed instead (plan section 11, open point).
9. **Household businesses are a kind of client, not a second ledger.** The legal entity keeps TT133 books underneath (nothing in the chart is added), and a `household_profile` row per entity and effective date holds what changes by law: the group, the PIT method, the trades it sells in, the exempt threshold and the rates as data with effective dates. The books of Circular 152 (S1a, S2a, S2b, S2c, S2d, S2e) are derived views over the entries, the stock moves and the bank lines, exported as spreadsheets; the household may adapt the forms as the circular allows. A return worksheet (01/CNKD) is a later step, after the accountant has confirmed forms, rates and thresholds.
10. **Ludy reads the marketplaces and does not write to them.** Stock write-back (updating a listing's quantity) is out of scope. One stock serves every channel because every channel's orders move the same item; pushing the number back is an Icebox item that needs an owner decision (a wrong number stops sales or oversells).
11. **The coworker proposes, code decides, a person approves.** The finance coworker matches lines to items, maps unknown report headers, flags items running low and drafts a reorder message to the supplier. Model calls go through `metered()` under new `AI_FEATURES` keys; the model never states a number it was not given, a person approves every posting and every message, and the core works with AI off.

## Consequences

- New tenant tables (`stock_item`, `stock_alias`, `stock_move`, `stock_period`, `stock_count` and its lines, `channel_order` and its lines, `channel_settlement` and its lines, `household_profile`) each need the two-step rule (an invariant-only PR with `PENDING_TABLES` and a row factory first), `org_purge`, manifest entries and `pnpm db:types`. The migrations are protected paths: the Platform PO reviews each.
- The sandbox parser gets two new document kinds (`marketplace_orders`, `marketplace_income`); the extraction contract is the worker's, so the Platform PO reviews it.
- A seller who sells only through one platform can run the whole flow from three downloaded files a month, with no credential given to Ludy.
- A report layout that changes breaks a profile, not the books: the staged read shows what it could not read and nothing is stored.
- Revenue from a marketplace is posted per settlement, not per order, so the sales ledger lags a shipment by the platform's payout period; the stock report and the order list show the shipment at once.

## Alternatives considered

- **Orders as sales documents.** Rejected: no invoice number or buyer, thousands of rows, and a second revenue for the same sale once the seller's own e-invoice is issued.
- **A perpetual moving average from the first slice.** Deferred: it needs a cost on every move at the time it is made, so a sale before the purchase invoice (common with marketplace orders) has no cost; the monthly average takes the cost when the month closes.
- **The API first.** Rejected: the survey found that most small sellers cannot connect an app themselves on at least one of the three platforms, and a shared app needs a partner registration and a security review Ludy does not have.
- **Blocking a sale beyond stock.** Rejected for orders (the platform has already taken the order); the month cannot be closed while an item is negative, which is where the people fix it.
- **A separate chart of accounts for household businesses.** Rejected: Circular 152 prescribes books, not a chart; deriving the books from TT133 entries keeps one ledger and one set of controls.

# The Ludy extension against the Chrome Web Store policy of 2026 (D1)

Issue #3180 (step D1 of [Meetings integrations](../plan/meetings-integrations.md), section 7), reviewed on 2026-11-15 at commit `c483edbc` of `main`. Scope: the extension (`apps/extension/**`), its store assets (`apps/extension/store/**`), the listing and privacy answers drafted in [the extension store runbook](../runbooks/extension-store.md), and the privacy page `https://ludy.so/privacy` (`apps/web/src/content/privacy-*.ts`). It is useful for D2 (Edge) and for the owner's submission (#2584); the things only a person can do are in the runbook, section "Policy review 2026: what only a person can do".

## What could and could not be read

**The store's own policy pages could not be read.** The session that wrote this review has no network route to `developer.chrome.com` (the name does not resolve: it is outside the session's allowed domains), so the program policies, the user data policy, the Limited Use text and the blog post on the 2026 changes were **not read**. No other route was tried (no mirror, no search-engine summary, no cached copy): a policy read second-hand is not evidence. To read them, `developer.chrome.com` has to be allowed in the session's network settings, or a person reads the pages (runbook, item P1).

What this review rests on instead:

- **The requirement as recorded in the issue and the plan** (research of 2026-11-14, not re-read here): the policy in force from 2026-08-01 requires the data an extension collects to be necessary for one disclosed purpose, every collection to be prominently disclosed, and the most common refusal is a permission that is asked and not used; the review points the issue lists (single purpose, permissions asked versus used, prominent disclosure before the first recording, the privacy policy saying where audio is processed and for how long, the data-safety answers, no remote code, limited use).
- **General knowledge of the long-standing store policies**, from before this session and **unchecked against the current text**: that is why each verdict below is "the extension against the requirement as summarized", and why every store-side wording is a person's item to confirm.
- **The code and the texts of this repository**, which were read: each piece of evidence below is a file and line of the commit above.

A verdict here is therefore a statement about the extension. It is not a statement that the store will accept it.

## The points, with verdict and evidence

| # | Policy point | Verdict | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Single purpose | **Pass, wording fixed** | One purpose: record the audio of a meeting in a browser tab with the microphone and upload it to the member's own Ludy workspace (`manifest.json` description, `_locales/*/messages.json`). The other things the popup does serve it: sign-in (`popup.ts:120`) and the org picker (`popup.ts:140`), the "starting soon" card that fills the meeting title (`agenda.ts`, B2) and live notes (`capture-run.ts:154`, ticked per meeting). `src/page-capture.ts` defines a generic capture contract that **no feature uses** and needs no permission the manifest lacks. **Gap found:** the single-purpose draft and the long description in the runbook named neither the calendar shortcut nor live notes; fixed (the drafts now say how both serve the one purpose) |
| 2 | Permissions asked and used | **Pass** (two items for a person) | `storage`: `popup.ts:18`, `background.ts:30,71`, `state.ts`. `identity`: `popup.ts:119-120` (`launchWebAuthFlow` only). Host `https://app.ludy.vn/*`: every request goes through `createClient(address, token)` (`api.ts:67`); there is no other `fetch`. `tabCapture`: `popup.ts:325` (`getMediaStreamId`), asked at `popup.ts:296` on the member's click when meetings is turned on, released at `popup.ts:308`. `offscreen`: `background.ts:18` (`USER_MEDIA`). Optional hosts `https://*/*`, `http://localhost/*`: `popup.ts:88` asks for **one origin** the member typed, removed again at `popup.ts:96`. No permission is asked and unused. The fixed list is in `src/manifest-policy.ts`, and CI fails a built manifest that differs from it (`scripts/check-manifest.mjs`; run for this review: "permissions match the fixed list"). **For a person:** (a) the broad optional pattern `https://*/*` is the one a reviewer may question; it is optional, asked for one origin at a time, and needed for self-hosted Ludy, and the justification is in the runbook; it cannot be narrowed without dropping self-hosting. The other collections are disclosed at the point of choice: live notes send audio while recording (`liveNotesHint`), the calendar shortcut says what it reads (`agendaNote`; it named only Google, although Outlook feeds the same card since B3, and now says "Google or Outlook"). The device name (browser and operating system) sent when connecting is disclosed on the privacy page and the runbook and, since #3273, also in the popup's sign-in step (`signInDeviceNote`); low sensitivity. | 4 | Privacy policy: where audio is processed and for how long | **Fixed, then pass** (lawyer must re-read) | The page exists at `/privacy` and `/en/privacy` and has the extension first: what is handled, the consent step, one address only, storage and encryption, who reads, the transcription provider and AI-off case, retention table (audio 30 days by default, video 7, text 12 months), rights. **Gaps found, fixed in `privacy-vi.ts` and `privacy-en.ts`, version 1.1 of 2026-11-15:** (a) the page said Ludy reads busy slots and **not titles** for the calendar, which stopped being true with B1 (#3165, Google) and B3 (#3167, Outlook); both now state, with the member's own switch, what is read and kept (name cut at 200, times, time zone, join address, number of invitees; never an invitee address, a description or an attachment), who reads it, when it is deleted, and that Outlook's permission is title-level from the connect; (b) live notes send audio **while recording**, which the page did not say; two rows were added to the extension's table, one for each |
| 5 | Data-safety answers of the listing (the "Privacy practices" tab) | **Pass** (answers updated, a person re-enters them) | The runbook's declaration (authentication information, personal communications, personally identifiable information: yes; website content, history, location, financial, health, user activity: no; the three certifications) still matches the code: the extension reads no page (`manifest-policy.ts` forbids content scripts), and the calendar list is received from the member's own Ludy account, not collected from a page. The "why" text of the two ticked categories now also names the calendar titles and the live-notes audio. The form is a declaration: the owner enters it and the lawyer (#2585) sees it first |

## What changed in this PR

- `apps/extension/src/strings.ts`: the note before turning meeting recording on says where the recording goes (point 3), and the calendar card's note says "Google or Outlook" (it named only Google). No manifest, permission or `package.json` change; the permission list is not loosened.
- `apps/extension/store/en-1-screenshot-1280x800.png` and `vi-1-…`: regenerated with `node scripts/store-art.mjs`, because the popup note is in them.
- `apps/web/src/content/privacy-vi.ts`, `privacy-en.ts`, `site.ts`: points 4 above, and one bullet under "Who can read a meeting" for the minutes a member sends to their own address (A6, #3269: off by default, only the parts the member ticked, the copy kept for sending is deleted once the delivery is done or given up, what was sent cannot be taken back); version 1.1, 2026-11-15. **This is live copy on the marketing site once merged:** the effective date in the page is the date of this change, so the owner moves it if the merge or the deploy is later, in the same three files.
- `docs/runbooks/extension-store.md`: the single-purpose draft, the long descriptions and the data-safety "why" rows brought in line, and the new section for what only a person can do.

## Follow-ups that are not blocking

- **Device name at sign-in:** the browser and operating system name sent when connecting is only in the privacy page; add one line to the popup's sign-in step if a reviewer asks (a P3 follow-up issue, #3273).
- **Edge (D2):** Microsoft's Edge Add-ons policies were not read either; this review does not apply to them.
- **The two policy re-reads** (the live policy text and the 2026 changes) are item P1 of the runbook; a differing requirement changes this table, not only the runbook.

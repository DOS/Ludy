# Payroll, social insurance and personal income tax for small teams: plan

> Plan for [#2498](https://github.com/DOS/Ludy/issues/2498), 2026-11-03, from the owner's question in the Accounting PO chat the same day ("why not build the missing features: output invoices, BHXH, etc?"). Design only: nothing here is built, no money is spent, no account was created, no credential was used. Every rule comes from public pages read on 2026-11-03 and carries where it comes from and how sure it is. The decision record is [ADR-0046](../adr/0046-payroll-for-small-teams.md); a Vietnamese summary for the owner is [payroll.vi.md](payroll.vi.md). Not legal advice: the accountant (#61) and a lawyer confirm the rules of section 5, and the plan says which.

> **Addendum 2026-11-04.** Payroll is its own app, not part of Kế toán (owner decision, [ADR-0046 addendum](../adr/0046-payroll-for-small-teams.md#addendum-2026-11-04-payroll-is-its-own-app)): a new module `hr` with its own app (working name Ludy Nhân sự), permissions `hr.payroll.read` and `hr.payroll.run` in place of the `finance.payroll.*` names below, and the month's posting handed to finance as an ordinary proposal that Kế toán approves from its totals. Where this plan says "in Kế toán" for the staff list, the pay sheet or the payslips, read "in the HR app".

## 1. Goal

A small business with staff runs payroll every month. Ludy today has no employee, no pay sheet, no payslip and no way to book salaries or the insurance and tax that come with them.

After this plan an owner or admin of a one-person company or a team of up to about 20 can, in Kế toán:

1. keep the staff list with each person's contract, pay components, dependents and payment account, under access that is narrower than the rest of the books;
2. open the month's pay sheet, which starts as last month's, enter what changed (overtime hours, unpaid days, a bonus, an advance), and see for each person the gross pay, the employee's insurance, the personal income tax with the family deductions, the net pay and the employer's cost, all computed by code from dated rules;
3. approve the month: the sheet becomes final, payslips come out as PDFs, and one posting proposal (accrual, insurance, tax) goes to the usual approval;
4. pay: a bank bulk-salary file per bank template, payment vouchers, and the remittances to the insurance agency and the tax authority matched against the bank statement like any other line;
5. declare: the monthly list for the insurance agency (the D02-LT layout), the quarterly and annual personal income tax data and schedules, and the withholding certificate data, as spreadsheets that the person files and signs in the official channels;
6. be helped by the finance coworker, which prepares the month from last month and what it notices, flags unusual changes and explains each line of a payslip in plain words, working only on a pseudonymized sheet.

No time-keeping, no leave balances, no employee portal in the first slices (section 7).

## 2. What the owner decided

- 2026-11-03: payroll, social insurance and personal income tax are missing features worth designing (the question in the Accounting PO chat; #2498 is the design step, the Accounting PO's order is #2499 first, then #2498).
- The Accounting PO allowed an ADR (0046) if needed.
- Standing rules that bind this design: AI is a coworker, never an "assistant", and a person approves money, messages and spend; model calls go through the AI budget meter; the core works with AI off; the open-source core runs with `docker compose up` and no DOS service; an agent never holds an org's credentials (the insurance portal and the tax portal need the employer's digital signature, which stays with the person).

## 3. Fit, and the AI part

- **Fit (AGENTS.md gate).** Anyone with staff does payroll at least monthly. One-person companies with one or two employees, and teams of up to about 20, are the target; several entities, payroll for hundreds, time-keeping integrations and HR suites are not (section 7).
- **Smallest slice.** One person, one legal entity, the defaults: add the employees, open the month, check, approve. The rules are in the product, the posting accounts follow the entity's chart, nothing to set up but the people.
- **AI part, named.** The finance coworker in the pay sheet: (a) prepares the month's sheet from last month and the changes it notices (a contract ending, a probation that reaches its end date, a new hire); (b) flags what looks wrong (a pay that moved a lot, a net pay below the minimum, a deduction over the legal cap, a dependent not registered, a cash payment of 5 million or more); (c) explains each line of a payslip in plain Vietnamese from the stored breakdown. It works on a **pseudonymized sheet** (an alias per person, amounts and component labels only, never a name, an identity number, a tax code, a bank account or an address). Model calls go through `metered()` under new `AI_FEATURES` keys (`finance.payroll_prepare`, `finance.payroll_flag`, `finance.payroll_explain`); the model never states a number that enters the sheet, code computes every figure, a person approves the month; AI off leaves a complete product. No coworker name appears in strings or stored data (`{finance}`).

## 6. The design

### 6.1 Employees and contracts

Tables per legal entity (the finance pattern, `le_scope`): `payroll_employee` (the person: name, birth date, the identity number `national_id`, the tax code, the BHXH number, address `pii`, the payment account `payment`; the identity number and the account **stored under the person's per-subject key**, ADR-0017), `payroll_employment` (effective-dated rows: contract type, start, end, probation end, pay components with amounts (base wage, allowances with a flag "named in the contract", other regular pay), the function that picks the expense account, workplace region, insured yes or no with the reason, tax treatment (progressive, 10%, commitment, non-resident), payment method), `payroll_dependent` (name, relation, birth date, registration date, the period the deduction applies; the tax code of the dependent when registered). Rows are effective-dated and append-only in the way contracts are: a raise is a new row, so a past month recomputes to the same figures. **No image of an identity document is stored.** An employee is not an org member and has no login.

### 6.2 The rules and the calculator

The rules of section 5 live in `@crove/l10n-vn` as dated data, one module per kind (minimum wage by region, insurance rates, the reference level, PIT brackets and deductions, exempt allowances), each row with its effective date, the cited text and the status `confirmed` or `commentary`; the calculator is a pure function of (the employment rows, the month's inputs, the rules in force on the month) with decimal arithmetic (`decimal.js` through the money helpers; never JS numbers for amounts). For each person and month:

1. **Gross** = base wage + allowances + other regular pay + overtime pay (the hourly rate times hours times the rate of Art. 98) + bonus, less unpaid days by the rule of the contract.
2. **Insurance wage** = the insurable pay (the base wage and the allowances the contract names with an amount) clamped to the floor and the cap in force on the month (BHXH and BHYT on the reference level; BHTN on the regional minimum wage); zero where the person is not insured or the 14-day rule applies.
3. **Employee insurance** = the insurance wage times 8%, 1.5% and 1% (by fund, each rounded in decimal to the dong); **employer insurance** = 14%, 3%, 0.5%, 3% and 1%; **union fee** = 2% of the insured payroll.
4. **Taxable income** = gross less the exempt parts (cash meal allowance up to the cap, the overtime exemption by the rule once confirmed, the others of row 13) less the employee insurance.
5. **PIT**: for a resident with a contract of 3 months or more, the monthly brackets applied to taxable income less the personal deduction, less 6.2 million per registered dependent, less the voluntary pension up to the cap; 10% of the payment for no contract or a contract under 3 months when the payment is 5 million or more (or the person asks), nothing with a commitment form on file; non-resident 20% is out of the first slice and refused with a message.
6. **Net** = gross less the employee insurance, less PIT, less advances and other agreed deductions, **checked against the 30% cap of Art. 129 and the minimum wage**; the employer's cost = gross + employer insurance + union fee.

The result of each step is stored on the line as a breakdown (the facts the payslip and the coworker's explanation read), so a payslip is a view of stored facts, not a recomputation. **A month cannot be approved while any rule it used is `commentary`**; the screen lists the rows that block it with their sources. Golden tests come from the accountant's worked examples (section 8) and a real small employer's anonymized month.

### 6.3 The month

`payroll_run` (month, legal entity, status `draft`, `approved`, `paid`, `closed`) with one `payroll_line` per employee. **Opening a month copies the employments in force and last month's recurring inputs**; the person enters what changed (overtime hours by type, unpaid days, bonus, advance, a new hire, an end date), typed or imported from a spreadsheet. The sheet shows each line's breakdown and the employer's total cost. **Approving** is R3 (a fresh second factor; owners and admins with `finance.payroll.run`; never an agent): it freezes the lines (append-only), produces the payslip PDFs, and creates the posting proposal. An approved month is corrected by an adjustment line in a later run, or by reversal of its posting while the period lock allows it.

A **payslip** is the pay statement of Labor Code Art. 95(3): the period, the components of the pay, overtime and night pay, each deduction with its amount, the net pay, with the employer's name; it is a PDF the person hands over. Sending it by email is a later, separately approved step (the message holds a salary).

### 6.4 Posting and payment

One proposal per run through the finance proposal path (R3, four-eyes where the org's policy needs it): the entries of row 18 with accounts from a **map per regime** (`tt133`, `tt99`) held as data (the unemployment insurance account is 3385 or 3386), the expense account chosen by each employee's function from the entity's chart, one line per account (never per person: the detail is on the run). Net pay, the insurance remittance and the tax remittance are vouchers whose bank lines are cleared by matching (#2385); Ludy **exports a bank bulk-salary file** per configurable template (columns: sequence, name, account, bank, amount, content; banks require their own layouts, which the org sets once) and **never initiates a payment**. A line paid in cash for 5 million or more is flagged (row 19).

### 6.5 Declarations as exports

- **Insurance agency**: the D02-LT in Decision 366's layout for new hires, changes and ends of the month, with the contribution wage per fund, as xlsx (and a PDF for the record). The person enters it on the portal or hands it to the intermediary; nothing is sent.
- **PIT, quarterly**: the figures of 05/KK-TNCN (headcount, taxable income, exempt income, tax withheld) and the schedules, per quarter; **annual**: the data of 05/QTT-TNCN and its schedules, for staff who authorized the payer; the **withholding certificate data** (03/TNCN) per person. As spreadsheets laid out like the forms. XML for the tax authority's tool follows only after a lawyer clears the XSD (section 8) and an accountant checks which layout is current.
- A person signs and files. Ludy never holds the employer's digital signature and never calls the agency's or the tax authority's portals.

### 6.6 Sensitive data

- **Access.** New permissions `finance.payroll.read` and `finance.payroll.run`, default roles owner and admin only (members and viewers see nothing, not even the existence of the module's data); every payroll query is `sensitive` (the audit row is written before the read). The person who runs payroll is the one who reads it.
- **Classes.** Identity number `national_id`, bank account `payment`, name, address and dependents' names `pii`, salary and pay components `sensitive` (chosen to restrict and audit, not as a legal conclusion: the survey could not show that the law names salary sensitive, row 21).
- **Erasure.** The personal-data registry lists every payroll column: the run lines and their amounts `keep_legal` until the retention a lawyer confirms (row 22); everything else `erase`. After the retention the per-subject key of the person is deleted and the append-only lines stay as amounts nobody can tie to a person. A request from a former employee is answered with what is kept and why (the Law's Art. 14).
- **Audit and logs** carry ids and counts, never a value of a classified column (the audit marker of plan section 5.2).
- **The model** sees the pseudonymized sheet only (section 6.7).
- **Where it lives.** A self-hosted install keeps employee data on the org's own server. For Ludy Cloud the lawyer's answers on the processor's exemption and on cross-border storage (section 8) come before payroll is switched on there.

### 6.7 The finance coworker's part

All proposals, all checked by code, all confirmed by a person; each under `metered(...)` and the finance AI budget.

- **Prepare** (`finance.payroll_prepare`): given a pseudonymized diff of last month (an alias per employee; the deltas of each component, the contract dates against the month), the coworker lists what probably changes: a probation that ends, a contract that ends, a new hire, a recurring overtime. It proposes inputs; a person accepts or edits each.
- **Flag** (`finance.payroll_flag`): code finds the anomalies (a pay that moved by more than a threshold, a net below the minimum, a deduction over the 30% cap, a dependent without registration, an employee insured above the cap, a month with a cash payment of 5 million or more, a rule that blocks approval) and the coworker puts each in one plain sentence.
- **Explain** (`finance.payroll_explain`): for one payslip, from the stored breakdown (labels and amounts only), it explains in plain Vietnamese how the net pay came out; every figure in the text must be one it was given (the rule of the payment reminder wording in `packages/agent`) or the text is refused.
- **Never**: a name, an identity number, a tax code, an account or an address is in a model call; the model computes nothing; it never presses approve.
- AI off: the sheet, the calculator, the payslips and the exports are complete.

## 7. What waits, and why

- **Time-keeping and leave balances.** The first slices take overtime hours and unpaid days as monthly inputs (typed or imported from a spreadsheet); a time-keeping system is a different product.
- **An employee portal and payslip by email.** Email holds a salary: a separately approved step.
- **Foreign employees** (the non-resident rate, the work permit, the 12-month insurance rule), **piece-rate and hourly staff**, **severance and job-loss calculation**, **the 13th month**, **annual finalization by authorization** beyond the data export.
- **XML for the tax authority's tool**, until the XSD licence and the current layout are settled.
- **Insurance claims** (sickness, maternity), and any integration with the agency's portal: no public API.
- **Several entities and hundreds of employees**: the Icebox.
- **Electronic labor contracts and the labor management register**: the register data (row 17) is a later export.

## 9. Risks and open points

- **The rules are unverified.** The plan gates the approval of a month on each rule's status, so a wrong commentary figure cannot reach a real payslip; the cost is that the first real run waits for the accountant's pull request.
- **Sources conflict** on the withholding for January to June 2026 (the new brackets apply to the whole 2026 tax period by the Law's Art. 29(2), but salary was withheld under the old table until July; the transition rule of Decree 253 Art. 70 is described only by commentary), on the deduction for health and education, on the PIT forms' names, on the filing portal, and on the household owner's insurance start date. Each is a rule row with both readings written down.
- **The reference level changed on 2026-07-01** and the minimum wage changes on 2027-01-01; a month that straddles nothing still needs its own row. Dated parameters are the answer; a test runs the calculator on the same employee in June and July 2026.
- **A pending amendment** of the Social Insurance Law (a Government bill reported for the October 2026 sitting) and a draft 0.3% work-accident rate may change rows within months: new rows, never edits.
- **Sensitive data in a public open-source repo**: fixtures use invented people and amounts; the golden files of a real employer are anonymized before they leave the employer's hands.
- **The tax return is the person's.** An export that the person files wrongly is the person's mistake; the exports say they are worksheets, and the layout version is named on them.
- **Ludy is not an I-VAN or a tax-services intermediary.** Nothing here files or signs; a later integration with a provider would need that provider's own terms.
- **A household business with employees** follows the same Labor Code and the insurance rules from 2025-07-01 for households that declare tax; the household books of the stock plan's household kind show the wage expense, and the owner's own pay is never a wage line.

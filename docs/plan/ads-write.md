## 1. What changes, and what does not

The approved v1 ([ads.md](ads.md) section 2, ADR-0040 point 2) is read only: Ludy shows spend and results next to the books. The owner now wants the owner-operator to **run ads from Ludy**: build a campaign with its ad sets and ads from Ludy's own posts and media library, launch it, watch it, change its budget, pause it. Hootsuite Ads is the reference: create and manage Facebook, Instagram, LinkedIn and X ads, a manual Boost of a published post, an Automated Boost, and ads on the content calendar.

What stays as decided and is **not reopened**:
- The Ads module (`ads`), the sibling package `@crove/ad-platform`, the connection and credential layer of ADR-0025, tenancy, the one write path and the job egress invariant.
- **Ads never posts to the books.** Spend still reaches Kế toán through intake (card and bank lines, and the platform invoices of slice D) and the books strip; Ludy only adds the *planned* spend beside the actual one (section 8).
- The read side and its adapters stay read only: the reader files keep their tests that no write endpoint is called (#2218). Writing is a separate adapter interface in a separate file with its own pinned endpoint list (section 9).

What changes: ADR-0040 point 2 ("read-only by construction", "any later write needs a new ADR: R3 commands, four-eyes, and an approval that names the amount") is answered by this amendment and ADR-0043, in the way that sentence asked for.

## 3. Money and approval

**Principle: nothing that can spend runs unless a human approved exactly that plan, within a cap the owner set, with a record.** The kernel's policy already has the pieces ([kernel plan](crove-os-kernel.md) section 4.7: R0 to R3, the derived-risk rule, P08, P10 to P13); this section maps the ads commands onto them.

### 3.1 Risk of each command

An action that can **start or raise spend** declares `sideEffect: 'financial'`, so it is **R3** by the derived-risk rule: it goes through a proposal, a human approver who is not an author of it (P10), a fresh second factor (P11) and the approver's own permission and legal entity (P12); above the org's money threshold it escalates one level (P08). What only reduces spend or changes nothing at the platform is R2 `external` or R1.

| Command | Risk | Who may run it | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| `ads.campaign.draft_save`, `draft_edit`, `draft_discard` | R1 | owner, admin, marketing | Local only, reversible |
| `ads.campaign.submit` (ask for approval) | R1, creates the proposal | owner, admin, marketing | Freezes the draft and its input hash |
| `ads.campaign.launch` (create and activate) | **R3 financial**, proposal | approver per P10 to P12 | The approval card names the amounts (3.2). Executed once (P13) |
| `ads.campaign.activate` / resume | **R3 financial** | approver | Spend restarts. Activating the objects of a launch is the last step of `ads.campaign.launch`, on its own approval (W2-4b); only a **resume** of something stopped or paused is this command's own R3 (W4) |
| `ads.budget.raise`, `ads.schedule.extend` | **R3 financial** | approver | Anything that raises the money a plan can spend |
| `ads.budget.lower`, `ads.schedule.shorten`, pause, archive | R2 external | owner, admin, marketing | Reduces or stops spend; no approval, always audited |
| `ads.pause_all` (kill switch, 3.6) | R2 external | owner, admin, marketing | Pauses everything Ludy launched in the org and blocks new launches until an owner clears the flag |
| `ads.cap.set` (spend caps, 3.3) | **R3**, owner only, `aal2` | owner | A cap raised is spend raised |
| `ads.write.enable` (org switch, 5.1), `ads.account.enable_write` | R3 | owner | Opens the write path at all |

A command that moves money declared below R3 fails registration (the derived-risk rule); the job egress invariant requires every command that feeds the write queue to declare an external effect.

### 3.2 What the approver sees and signs

The proposal's card (the approval screens of the kernel) shows, for every ad set: the objective, the **daily or lifetime budget in the account's currency**, the schedule, therefore the **largest amount the plan can spend** (lifetime budget, or daily budget times the days, summed over the ad sets), the audience summary (location, age, gender, interests), the placements, and a preview of each ad. The approval binds the **input hash** of this plan (the command's input is hashed under the deployment's key, #1732): any change to a budget, a date or a creative after submission is a new proposal. Approving does not give Ludy a standing right to spend: only to run this plan once (P13).

Four-eyes (P10) applies when two or more eligible humans exist. **The one-person-company path:** an org whose only eligible human is the owner may approve its own plan, and the audit row says `self_approved`; nothing else is relaxed: a fresh second factor (P11) is still required, the card still names the largest amount, and the caps still bind. An owner with no verified second factor cannot switch writing on (5.1) until they enrol one. Once a second eligible human joins, the owner can no longer approve a plan they wrote. An agent never approves (ADR-0029).

### 3.3 Spend caps

Per ad account and per org: a **daily cap** and a **monthly cap**, in the account's currency, set by the owner (`ads.cap.set`, step-up). **The default is no cap and therefore no launch**: until the owner sets a cap, every launch is refused with `ADS_SPEND_CAP_NOT_SET` (recommendation, Q3). **One cap is enough** (decided by the Marketing PO on 2026-11-02 from Q3 and the "smallest slice" gate of AGENTS.md): at least one cap in force, daily or monthly, the account's or the org's, in the account's currency; the plan's largest amount is checked against every cap that exists, the daily budget against each daily cap and each calendar month of the flight against each monthly cap (W1a's local validation, which says `no_cap`, `daily_cap_exceeded`, `monthly_cap_exceeded` or `cap_currency`). At approval time and again at execution the command computes the **committed spend**: for every live or scheduled campaign of the account, its daily budget times the days left in the period, or its lifetime budget left, plus this plan's. A plan that would exceed a cap is refused (`ADS_SPEND_CAP`) with the numbers. Meta's own account spending limit is read and shown beside Ludy's caps [U: the field and its permission]; Ludy's caps are a second fence, never a replacement of the platform's.

**The execution check runs under a lock.** The check at approval only warns early; the one at execution is authoritative. The launch step takes a per-org transaction advisory lock, recomputes the committed spend from `crove_ads` rows and, in the same transaction, writes the plan's commitment before any platform call. Two launches approved together serialise: the second sees the first's commitment and is refused if both would pass a cap. A commitment is released only when its objects are archived or their schedule has ended. A test launches in parallel and asserts that committed spend never passes a cap.

**Caps have a currency.** A cap is a decimal string in the currency's own units (dong, or dollars with cents: the units a person types and the Ads screen shows, at most two decimals), with an ISO 4217 currency code, never a JS number; the launch converts a platform's minor-unit budgets with decimal.js before it compares. Each cap carries its own currency. An account cap is in the account's currency (refused at `ads.cap.set` otherwise, and before the account's first update, when its currency is unknown). An org cap is in any ISO currency (VND by habit); a launch is refused with `ADS_CAP_CURRENCY` unless every org cap that applies is in the account's currency, since converting would add a rate the approver did not see, so it is a later amendment. The currency of an org cap can be changed by setting the cap again.

### 3.4 Exactly-once calls to the platform, and reconcile

Every platform call that creates or changes something goes through the kernel's `external_call` ledger with a **deterministic key** (`ads.launch:<launch id>:<step>`, steps in order: campaign, each ad set, each creative, each ad, then the activation), the same pipeline as publishing a post. A step runs at most once per attempt; a timeout or a lost answer leaves it `ambiguous`, and a **reconcile job asks the platform before anything is retried**.

- Meta has no idempotency key for creating campaign objects [U], so every object Ludy creates carries a tag in its name (`ludy:<short id>`), and reconcile lists the account's objects by that tag to find one that was created but not recorded.
- Objects are created **paused**, so a launch that stops half way has spent nothing; activation is the last step and its own call.
- A failed or stopped launch shows which step it stopped at; **retry continues from there**, discard archives the paused objects (R2).

### 3.5 Audit, undo and agents

- Every command writes an audit row with the ids, the amounts and the states, never the creative text (the campaign and ad names are business text; they follow the data classes of the manifest).
- There is no undo of money already spent: the controls are the approval before, the caps, the pause (always allowed), and the platform's own limit.
- **What an agent may do:** write **drafts** (R1), which reach a human as a proposal like any agent work; **never launch, activate, resume, raise or extend, set a cap, or enable writing.** This amends the write allowlist of ADR-0029 for the draft commands only (ADR-0043 point 7, the Marketing PO's recommendation, which the owner may reverse).

### 3.6 The kill switch

`ads.pause_all` (R2, owner, admin and marketing members, no proposal and no second factor, because it only stops spend) pauses every campaign and ad set Ludy launched in the org, found from the recorded object ids and checked against the `ludy:` name tag, as `external_call` steps keyed `ads.pause_all:<run id>:<object>` so a double click pauses once. It sets the org flag `launches_blocked`: while set, every launch, activation and resume is refused, and only an owner clears it (R3, step-up). Turning the org switch off (5.1) runs the same pause first. The Ads screen offers it as one clearly marked button; the audit row counts the objects paused and lists any the platform refused with its reason.

## 4. Meta's objects, states and limits

[C] unless tagged. A campaign is created with a name, an objective and `special_ad_categories` (required; `NONE` when none), under `POST /act_<id>/campaigns`; an ad set with its budget, schedule, targeting, optimization goal and billing event under the ad set edge; an ad creative (for a post-based ad from a published post, the story of that post [U: whether `object_story_id` is supported for every objective and placement; one Meta page says it is not supported for Click-to-Messenger]) and an ad under the ads edge. The current objective names are the "outcome" ones (`OUTCOME_TRAFFIC`, `OUTCOME_ENGAGEMENT`, `OUTCOME_LEADS`, `OUTCOME_SALES` and others) [C]. Budgets are in the account currency's smallest unit and have a minimum per currency [U].

**States.** An object's `effective_status` moves through `IN_PROCESS` (post-processing after a create or edit), `PENDING_REVIEW`, `ACTIVE`, `PAUSED`, `WITH_ISSUES` and `DISAPPROVED`, with the reasons in `review_feedback` and `issues_info` [C]. Ludy reads them back with the existing hourly sync (a new read of object states, never a write) and shows each ad as in review, rejected (with Meta's reason, in words) or active. A rejected ad is never re-submitted automatically. How long a review takes is [U]; the screen says "in review" and when Ludy last asked.

**Rate limits.** Write calls count against the same tier caps as reads, and the Limited tier's are low [C for the score cap, U for how many creates one launch may make an hour] (ads.md section 5.3): a launch of one campaign with a few ad sets and ads is about ten calls. Ludy spaces the steps, backs off for the time a limit names, and the app never retries in a loop.

**Access.** See section 5.2. On the Limited tier writes to the owner's **own** ad accounts are allowed with `ads_management` [C]; whether the tier's write limits are enough for a real launch is [U] and the first thing the W2 live check records.

## 5. Permissions and access

### 5.1 The org switch, which replaces the refusal guard

A read-only connection and a read-write one are different things, so write access is a **separate, explicit step**, in three layers:

1. **Deployment:** the platform app. The same Meta app may serve (as for reading), but a **separate app for writing is the recommendation** (Q7 of ADR-0043): `ads_management` on the app that also posts to Pages widens what a leaked Pages token can do, and a failed App Review for writing must not block reading.
2. **Org switch** `ads.write` (off by default; `ads.write.enable`, owner, step-up, audited). Off: the connector keeps the guard of #2275 and **refuses any token that holds `ads_management` or `business_management`** (read-only orgs keep refusing write scopes). On: the connector for that org asks Meta for `ads_read` and `ads_management` (and the Page permissions a post-based ad needs [U]) and **accepts** them, recording the full set on the connection.
3. **Ad account:** `write_enabled` per ad account (owner), default off, so one account can stay read-only inside an org that writes. Connecting for write is a **new connection step** ("Cho phép Ludy chạy quảng cáo trên tài khoản này"), never an upgrade of a connection in place: the person goes through Meta's dialog again and the screen shows the account as able to write.

**The write token is its own credential:**
- *Sealed separately:* a second sealed credential of the connection (ADR-0025), apart from the read token, opened by the worker alone and only inside a write job, so a leak of the read path cannot spend.
- *Require App Secret:* every write call carries `appsecret_proof`, and the write app has "Require App Secret" on at Meta, so a copied token is useless without the app secret.
- *Revoked at Meta on disconnect or switch-off:* the worker removes the app's permissions at Meta (the writer's one call that is not about an ad object, named in its endpoint list) and then deletes the sealed copy. A failed revoke is retried and shown as not yet revoked; the sealed copy stays until it succeeds.
- *Queued writes stop when the switch goes off:* the write job re-checks the org switch and the account's write flag before every step. Switching off cancels every launch not started and every step not run; a step in flight finishes and is recorded; created objects stay paused.

The guard of #2275 is therefore not removed but **made conditional on the org switch**; its tests stay and gain the switch-on path. The Ads screen shows, per account, whether the token can only read or can also run ads (the `extraScopes` line of #2275 becomes a clear "read only / can run ads" state).

### 5.2 Meta access, and what only JOY can do

All of this is an `owner-action` (AGENTS.md): no agent signs in or submits a review.

- **Own ad accounts, the owner's classic app, Limited tier:** standard access to `ads_read` and `ads_management` is enough for an app that manages only its owner's accounts [C]. Needed: the person connecting has a role on the app, the app is in development mode or its permissions are live for that person, and the ad account has a payment method on Meta's side. This is what JOY can do **today** with the existing app, after W0 to W2 are built.
- **Other businesses' accounts (Ludy Cloud):** Advanced access to `ads_management` (and `business_management` if used) through **App Review**, which needs **Business Verification** of the portfolio behind the app, a screencast of the whole create-and-launch flow, a privacy policy URL, a data deletion callback (already planned for reading, ads.md section 9), and a usage history (Meta's older guidance: 1,500 calls in 15 days, a newer page says 500 [C, disagree]) [C]. Whether Ludy's approval step (a human approves before spend) helps the review is [U]. Steps for JOY, in order: (1) decide Q5 on timing; (2) confirm the business portfolio is verified; (3) create the separate write app (Q7) with the Marketing API product; (4) record the screencast from the W2 build on the owner's own account; (5) submit; (6) answer Meta's follow-up.
- **Meta's advertising policies and ad review** apply to every ad the owner makes; Ludy shows Meta's rejection reasons and never tries to evade a review.
- **Vietnamese advertising law** (the Law on Advertising and its decrees: restricted products and claims, content rules) [S, not read here] applies to the owner's ads; Ludy does not check ad content against it. A lawyer reads it before Ludy offers templates or suggests wording (the AI-written ad copy of a later slice, if any).

### 5.3 Roles

New permissions, none granted today (`roles.ts` grants nothing for ads): `ads.campaign.draft` (owner, admin, marketing members), `ads.campaign.approve` (owner, admin; the holders of the approval permission, P12), `ads.campaign.manage` (pause, lower, archive: owner, admin, marketing), `ads.cap.set` and the two switches (owner only). The author of a plan cannot approve it (P10); finance roles and viewers read.

## 6. Personal data

- **Targeting stores none.** Location (country, region, city and a radius), age range, gender, language, interests (Meta's interest ids and names) and placements are **parameters of an audience, not people**; no table holds a person. They are classed as ordinary business text in the module manifest. The audience size estimate Meta returns is an aggregate.
- **Creatives** are the org's own posts and media (already stored by Social and the file store); Ludy copies the text and the file ids into the draft, and the platform stores the rest.
- **Custom audiences are personal data and are a separate decision (Q6).** A customer list (emails, phones) uploaded to Meta is a transfer of third parties' personal data to a platform, under the Law on Personal Data Protection (Law 91/2025/QH15, Decree 356/2025/NĐ-CP, as the [privacy notice](../legal/privacy.md) cites them): it needs a legal basis, a notice, a processor agreement and a lawyer's reading. **Recommendation: out of the first slices.** Lookalikes depend on them; Conversions API and pixel events send customer events to Meta and are out for the same reason; **lead ad forms collect personal data on the platform and are out**. If JOY wants them, a further amendment and ADR open with the lawyer first.
- **No breakdowns** of results by age, gender or region (ads.md section 9) stay out: the aggregates a campaign reports are what the screens show.
- The privacy notice gains the new data category (campaign drafts and targeting parameters) with the wording of [#2247](https://github.com/DOS/Ludy/issues/2247).

## 7. Tenancy, credentials, egress

- The same rules as reading: every table has `org_id`, `PRIMARY KEY (org_id, id)`, RLS and the legal-entity scope; no foreign key outside `crove_ads` and `crove_kernel`; no cross-module read (the creative comes from the app, composed from a Social query, and is **copied into the draft at submission**, so Ads reads no Social table).
- **Credentials:** the write token is the connection's sealed token (ADR-0025), opened by the **worker alone**; the API never calls the platform. The write adapter is handed the token only inside the launch job.
- **Egress:** the write queue (`ads-write`) declares the same `provider:ad_platform` target; every command that feeds it declares R2 or R3 and an external effect (the job egress invariant). The write adapter's fetch is bound to `graph.facebook.com` over https exactly as the reader (#2275).
- The invariant suite grows rows for the new tables (RLS matrix, as in #2237 and #2265), a test that **no agent context writes** the launch tables, a redaction test (no token anywhere), and a test that the write adapter's source names only the endpoint list of section 9.

**W0a-1 as built (the state, the stops, the guards).** Two tables, `crove_ads.ad_org_setting` (the org's `write_enabled` and `launches_blocked`, one row per org by a unique key; the commands also take a lock on first use) and `crove_ads.ad_spend_cap` (a row per cap change: scope `org` or `account`, period, amount, currency, who set it and when; append-only), and `ad_account.write_enabled`. Org-wide, so neither table names a legal entity; owners and admins read them through `ads.write.state`. Direct commands, all of them what only stops spend: `ads.write.disable` (R2, owner: every account flag off and launches blocked), `ads.account.disable_write` (R2, owner) and `ads.pause_all` (R2, owner and admin: blocks launches at once; **there is nothing launched to pause yet**, the pausing of objects comes with the launch, W2). What opens the way to spend (the org switch on, an account enabled, a cap set or raised, launches opened again) is a proposal with P10 and the one-person path, as ADR-0043 point 1 says, and comes with W0a-2; **no direct command does it, and a test pins that none exists**. The database guards the tables whatever path reaches them (`crove_ads.switch_guard`, `cap_guard`, fired for every role): a write switch turned on, on a row made or changed, or launches unblocked, needs a signed-in owner **or the execution of the approved proposal of its own command** (`crove_ads.approved_execution`, a second branch that is present but **stubbed closed** in W0a-1 and opened by W0a-2 behind the approval gate's marker, with tests both ways; never a plain admin or member context); turning one off or blocking launches, an owner or admin; an agent, the system or any other context is refused either way; the switches stamp who changed them and when, and a cap row is inserted by a signed-in owner alone with its id, who set it and when stamped by the server, so "the latest cap" is the newest server-stamped id and a crafted future id cannot stay the cap for good. A live agent run cannot do any of it (a test enters an agent context as the worker does). The module permissions of 5.3 are not defined yet: the commands use the base roles (owner, admin) until W1's drafting and approving commands give a permission something to name. Nothing calls a platform; the Ads screen shows the switches, the per-account flags and the caps, and holds the stop controls, for owners and admins.

**W0a-2 as built (the opening, as proposals).** The commands that open the way to spend, `ads.write.enable`, `ads.account.enable_write`, `ads.cap.set` (set or raise) and `ads.launches.unblock`, are R3 `financial` **proposal commands** (`access: 'proposal'`, run only by an approval) asked for through R1 preparers (`ads.write.prepare_enable`, `ads.account.prepare_enable_write`, `ads.cap.prepare_set`, `ads.launches.prepare_unblock`; only an owner can ask to open spend, never an admin or a member (ADR-0043 points 2, 3 and 7a: the switches and the caps are the owner's; an owner or an admin approves, and never the one who asked, except on the one-person path); one request of a kind at a time, the checks of the old direct commands now run at the request and again at the approval). The approval policy is owner or admin, no approval permission, four-eyes with the one-person path (P10), no ledger (`approval.ts` and `crove_kernel.approval_command_policy`, kept equal by the parity invariant); `APPROVAL_POLICY_VERSION` is 3. The second way through the database guards is open: `crove_ads.approved_execution(command, match, amount)` answers true only for a proposal of the org for exactly that command in the state `approved` (which exists only inside the approving transaction), decided by the signed-in user of this context in this transaction, whose payload names the change being written (the account, or the cap's scope, period, currency and amount); a system or agent context never passes, and a raw statement outside that transaction finds no approved row. Lowering a cap is the owner's direct R2 `ads.cap.lower` (strictly below the cap in force, in its currency); stopping stays as in W0a-1. Cap rules unchanged: amounts are decimal strings above zero with at most two decimals, an account cap in the account's currency after its first update, a cap that is not above the one in force is refused at the request (lower it instead). The Ads screen asks for each change and lists what waits in the Inbox; the Inbox shows an ads proposal as "what changes" with an Approve that asks for the second factor (P11), and keeps it out of bulk approval.

**W2-1 as built (the revoke at the platform; #2296).** The owner approved on 2026-11-03 that Ludy keeps a **sealed copy of a closed ads connection's token for seven days at most, only to remove the person's permissions at the platform** (ADR-0025's amendment of that date; step one of the invariant route is #2486). **The hold is the database's, so no path can skip it**: `crove_ads.token_hold` is a BEFORE DELETE trigger on `crove_kernel.credential`; whatever path drops the credential of an ads write connection, or of an ads read connection whose recorded scopes hold `ads_management` or `business_management`, copies the envelope into `crove_ads.ad_token_seal` first, in the same statement (so a live credential is never copied), and opens one row of `crove_ads.ad_token_revoke` (one per connection) that records the grantor's keyed hash from the account the connection served. The switch-off, an account's withdrawal or disconnect, the generic `kernel.channel.disconnect`, the rollback of a refused link and the sweep's close of a member who left all go through it (a connection made again for the same account is the same row and replaces its credential in place: nothing is closed, so nothing is held); **not held**: a platform's own data deletion (the person already removed the app, so the deletion request is honoured at once), a Meta read connection with no recorded spend permission (the fail-safe close of the reading connections is local, as W0b-2 says), every provider outside Meta's ads providers and the sandbox (the owner approved the copy for Meta; another platform needs its own decision), a closed org (no setting a session can make switches the hold off; the hold takes the org's row FOR SHARE, so a close racing the purge finishes first or finds the org closed; an org in offboarding still holds). The seal is definer-only machinery (no grant to any role, RLS on); the state is read by tenant roles and written by definers alone. **One sweep a minute** (`ads-revoke`, a schedule with no data and `retryLimit` 0, so no id or token sits in a job and only the policy retries; egress `provider:ad_platform`, nothing else): (1) obligations past seven days or ten tries are settled and their copies wiped (`token_revoke_expire`, which the agent sweep, a schedule with no egress, runs too, so the age limit does not depend on this job); (2) an open write connection whose connector is no longer an owner or admin (removed, demoted, disabled) is closed with the kernel's system command `kernel.channel.close_orphan`, so the token nobody can use any more is held, revoked and wiped instead of sitting in Ludy (`write_orphans`); (3) due obligations are claimed with a lease of ten minutes (`token_revoke_due`); **a revoke is the person's, for the app, in every org** (Meta's permission belongs to the pair), so before any call a boolean-only definer (`token_grant_shared`) says whether another live connection with the same grantor hash exists in any org, and then no call is made and the obligation ends as `shared_grant` (the owner's decision of 2026-11-03: the call is not narrowed to one permission); otherwise the copy is opened through **the one worker-only definer** `token_seal_get` (EXECUTE to `crove_worker` alone, in the org's system context, for a claimed obligation; it counts the try as it opens, consumes the claim, and refuses a copy that is not pending, is seven days old or has used its ten tries; it refuses another session even if granted; it does not look at the connector, so **a member's removal does not wipe an unrevoked copy**: the revoke runs first), opened with the broker under the binding stored on the obligation and handed to the narrowed `AdRevoker` of its provider (`channels.adRevokers`, built by `adRevokersOf`: a frozen object with `revokeToken` and no other call, which sends one `DELETE /me/permissions` and nothing else; the write app's secret proves a write token, the read ads app's a read token), and the outcome goes through the system command `ads.token_revoke.record`, which applies the policy of `modules/ads/src/token-revoke.ts`. **The policy**: success, or the person having removed the app (error 190 with subcode 458), is done and wipes the copy; any other refusal of the token (463, 467, 460, a 190 with no subcode, 102), and a copy that does not open, mean nothing can be revoked from the copy and the grant may still stand: wiped at once and given up as `unrevocable` ("revoke by hand", never "revoked"); **a deployment with no revoker for the provider or no credential broker** (a worker started without the Meta app's secret or the credential key) is a fault of its configuration, not a finding about the token, so the sweep opens nothing and records nothing: the copy stays pending (the claim runs out in ten minutes and it is claimed again, no try counted), a configuration fix restores the revoke, and if it is never fixed the seventh day ends it as given up by age ("revoke by hand"); an error of the deployment (a wrong app secret or proof, 100 or 200) is never read as a 190 and is a counted failure; a failure keeps the copy and sets the next try (backoff 1, 5, 15, 60 minutes, 3, 6, 12 hours, then a day, or the platform's own time when later); the tenth try or the seventh day gives up, wipes the copy and the Ads screen says to remove the app in the platform's settings. **A new connection of the same account by the same member cancels the pending revoke** in the transaction that seals its credential (the person has given the permission again, and the revoke would cut it; another member's connection leaves the first member's revoke to go on); a reseal of the key does not. With no grantor recorded (a refused link leaves one) the same member's other live connection of the same provider, in any org, is taken to be theirs and the call is not made. An org in offboarding still holds (a disconnect in that window is revoked before the purge); the purge's own credential drops hold too (the hold reads no setting, so nothing a session sets switches it off, and the purge deletes the copies and the state as its own step after the connection rows), so what is still connected when it runs is not revoked; the erasure of an account whose write grant is another person's than the requester's closes it as `account_erased` and holds. The age sweep leaves a try that was opened and not yet answered to answer (ten minutes), and `ads.token_revoke.record` touches its own obligation only. The database's guard holds the same rules whatever path writes the row: done needs a try (but for a shared grant and a reconnect, which make no call), give-up by attempts the tenth try, give-up by age the seventh day (a few minutes of slack for a clock; by the obligation or by the copy), a settled obligation never changes, and **the copy is deleted in the statement that ends the obligation**. **Ids and codes only** in audit, events and logs (the record names the obligation, the state, the count and the platform's error code), and the secret lives in one function's scope. The org purge deletes both tables as a step of its own after the connection rows, the copy before the state it references. **The screen**: `ads.write.state` carries `tokenRevokes` (open ones and the ones that gave up in the last 30 days, owners and admins), and the card says "removing the app's permission at Meta, tried n times, next try at …" or, after the limit, how to remove the app by hand; the old note that said "you also remove the app" now says Ludy does it and keeps only an encrypted copy for seven days. **Tests**: `invariant/ads-token-seal.db.test.ts` (no role has a privilege on the copy, the worker definers and their refusals, the open in the system context with the claim and the counted try, the refusals at seven days and ten tries, the hold on every kind of close and the cases it skips, the grantor, the guard's rules, the age sweep with no job, the lease, a member's removal, the reconnect, the shared grant, the purge and a close racing it), `invariant/ads-token-seal-source.test.ts` (one file opens the copy through one call, nothing else names the table, the job carries no data and only the revokers and the broker of the worker deps, nothing logs the secret), `ads-token-revoke.db.test.ts` (every close path, the sweep end to end on the sandbox, failures and their schedule, the counted try after a crash, nothing that can be revoked, the shared grant, the reconnect, the orphan connection, ids only in audit, events, idempotency keys and jobs, the screen) and `ad-platform/test/revokers.test.ts` and `meta-write.test.ts` (the narrowed view has the one frozen method; the one request it can send whatever Meta answers; each Meta token is proved with its own app's secret; 190/458 against the other refusals). **Deliberately not in W2-1**: the credential key's rotation (the previous key must stay seven days, see the credential-key runbook; a copy that no longer opens is given up as unrevocable), an owner's "try now" (a copy is never opened outside the sweep), the pause of launched objects before the revoke (W2-4: until then no launch path is enabled, so a revoke-only wind-down is consistent), and a connection replaced in place by another person (the previous grantor's token is overwritten without a copy; the connect command knows only the new envelope).

**W2-2, step one (the launch ledger; #2296).** The ledger is `crove_ads.ad_launch`, one row per approved plan (one per draft), and the commitment that plan holds against the spend caps. This step lists it as pending in the RLS matrix, with a row factory, and changes nothing else (the table, its guard and the commands are step two, so the factory has to be on main first, as for W2-1, #2486). **The shape the factory relies on**: tenant table `(org_id, id)`, in the account's legal entity (`ad_account_id`, `legal_entity_id`: trigger `b_legal_entity`, the composite foreign key and the RESTRICTIVE `le_scope` policy, as the draft); `draft_id` (the approved draft, unique), `proposal_id` (the approved proposal, stamped by the guard, no foreign key), `plan_hash` (`sha256:` and 64 hex, the hash the proposal carries), `tag` (generated: `ludy:` and ten characters of the id), `state` (queued, launching, live, needs_attention, stopped, failed, cancelled), `attempt`, `step`, `currency`, `daily_budget`, `start_on`, `end_on`, `amount` (always the budget times the days of the flight, and a flight is at most 90 days: CHECKs), `commit_until` (the last day it still commits; it only shortens, and is cleared when nothing is committed), `stop_reason`, and who and when. **W2-2 as built (the launch ledger and the caps under the lock; #2296).** Nothing launches in this slice: no queue, no job, no platform call (W2-3 adds the writer, W2-4 the pause on switch-off, and no launch path is switched on before W2-4). **The ledger** is `crove_ads.ad_launch`, one row per approved plan (one per draft): the account and its legal entity, the draft, the approved proposal (stamped the attempt, the account's currency, the daily budget, the first and last day of the flight, the largest amount (a CHECK: the budget times the days, and a flight is at most 90 days) and `commit_until`, the last day the launch still commits. **The row is made by the approval and nothing else**: `ads.campaign.launch`, in the transaction that approves a plan, checks the caps and writes the row, and the database's guard (`crove_ads.launch_guard`, a definer fired for every role in a context of the org) accepts an insert only inside the execution of that plan's approved proposal (`approved_execution`: the draft, the hash, the account, the currency and the amount, and the budget and the days read from the proposal's own plan), for the approved draft of that account and amount, while the org's switch and the account's are on and launches are not blocked. **The caps are checked under the org's lock**, the one every ads command that changes the switches or the caps already takes (`lockedSetting`): the command reads, after taking it, the caps in force and every launch that still commits (the definer `crove_ads.launch_commitments`: ids, a currency, a budget as text and two dates, for owners, admins and the system, across the org's entities, since an org cap counts every account and an approver limited to some entities must not miss the others' launches), judges the plan against them in decimal (`modules/ads/src/launch-caps.ts`: a daily cap day by day, a monthly cap month by month, the account's caps against the account's launches, an org cap against every launch, a cap in another currency left to W1's `cap_currency`; one cap, daily or monthly, is enough, and a plan is judged against every cap that exists) and writes the row; two launches approved together queue on the lock, and the second sees the first. A plan over a cap is refused with `ADS_SPEND_CAP` and the numbers (the cap, what is committed, what the plan adds, the first day or the month). **The request for approval runs the same check** as an early warning (`ads.campaign.prepare_launch`); the approval's is the authoritative one. **A commitment** holds the budget on every day from the first day to `commit_until`, which starts at the last day of the flight and only moves earlier (a launch that is stopped keeps what ran; one cancelled or failed before it was activated commits nothing: `commit_until` is cleared); a released commitment never comes back. **The state columns** are the tenant roles' to update (a column grant), and the guard holds every change: a state moves only along its graph (queued to launching or cancelled; launching to live, failed, needs_attention or stopped; needs_attention to launching, failed or stopped; live to stopped), the writer's moves, the step and the attempt count are the system's, stopping and cancelling are an owner's, an admin's or the system's (written so a context with no role cannot pass), a live launch is stopped with what ran committed, a final state never changes, and what a member or a viewer writes into a state column is refused from every state. **Cancelling**: `ads.launch.cancel` (R2, owners and admins) releases a queued launch at once; `ads.write.disable`, `ads.account.disable_write`, `ads.account.disconnect` and `ads.pause_all` release the queued launches of the org or the account (the audit row counts them), so a plan approved before a switch-off cannot start after a reconnect; the draft stays approved, and a new plan is a new draft and a new approval. The Ads builder shows an approved plan's launch (its amount held against the caps, from the first day to `commit_until`) with the cancel button for owners and admins. **Not in W2-2**: the writer, the job, the object states and the screens of W2-3 and W2-4; the re-read of the caps before the writer's first platform call (W2-3); a draft approved before this table (W1) has no ledger row and the writer launches only a draft that has one. **Tests**: `invariant/ads-launch-ledger.db.test.ts` (the grants, the guard's insert and every move, a member's and a viewer's refused changes from every state, the arithmetic constraints, `launch_commitments`, the caps' append-only history, the owner-only switches, the approval judged on the people of the org now), `ads.db.test.ts` (the approval's ledger row, the committed spend at the request and at the approval with the numbers, five approvals at the same moment, cancel and the stops), `modules/ads/test/launch-caps.test.ts` (the arithmetic and a property over random flights).

**W2-3, step one (the writer and the launch job; #2296).** This step lists the one new tenant table, `crove_ads.ad_object`, as pending in the RLS matrix with a row factory, and classifies the one new worker field in the job-egress invariant (`channels.adWriters`, what the launch job may call: the writers of `@crove/ad-platform`, the sandbox's reaching nothing and the Meta one the Graph API host). Tenant roles read it and update the status columns only (a column grant); a row is inserted by the system's record step of a running launch and nothing else, so the matrix has no own-org insert control for it.

**W2-3 as built (the writer and the launch job; #2296).** A queued launch is run by the job of the queue `ads-launch` and ends as **objects created, paused**; nothing in this slice un-pauses anything (W2-4 activates, behind the same caps re-check and stop conditions, with its own approval and stop path). `ads.launch.record` (R1) settles the row (`succeeded` with the platform's id and the object's row in `crove_ads.ad_object` in the same transaction, the object's name being the one the plan and the tag make; `failed` with the platform's kind and code, the launch `needs_attention`; `ambiguous` for an answer that cannot be told; `missing` re-arms the row for one more call) and moves the launch's `step` (the level reached, then `paused`). **The job** (`apps/kernel/src/ads-launch.ts`): begin;

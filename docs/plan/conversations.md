## 1. The decision and what it means

Owner decision of 2026-11-03 (recorded on #2213, built from #2437), which replaces the split of 2026-11-01 (comments in Social, private messages in Service): **the inbox follows Crisp and Intercom, not Zendesk.**

- **One model, the conversation** (`crove_kernel.conversation`), for every customer interaction: chat (Messenger, Instagram DMs, Zalo OA chat; WhatsApp later), comments on the org's own posts (a Facebook Page post, an Instagram media) and customer email, in and out.
- **One inbox, Hộp thư.** Every conversation is a chat thread with the tabs **New, Pending and Resolved**. An email shows as chat bubbles too (the quoted history folded, section 6.7). Staff reply in the same box, whatever the channel; the reply box names the channel.
- **No cases, tickets or SLA timers.** A conversation that needs internal follow-up links to a task (`crove_kernel.task`, section 4); it never becomes a numbered record, and nothing counts reply time against a promise.
- **The post page keeps its Bình luận tab**: the same conversations, filtered to the post. It is a view of the inbox, not a second inbox, so a comment has one unread count and one place to answer it.
- **One core underneath**, so the inbox and the posting side each run from the same data and neither holds a second copy.
- **The AI coworker in Hộp thư** (Dy in Ludy Engage, who also covers Khách hàng & Bán hàng; Uy covers Marketing, Bài đăng and Quảng cáo; the names stay behind the roster map and the i18n keys, DESIGN.md 1.1, and the as-built sections below still say Vy) sorts the intent of a conversation, suggests the matching contact profile and drafts a reply that a person sends (section 5.6). No auto-send, and the core works with AI off.

The plan said the opposite at first: [social.md](social.md) "Not in Social: comments and messages" and kernel plan section 5.7 "Messages and comments land in Service's inbox; Social only publishes". Both changed on 2026-11-01 and change again for the decision above (section 14).

What exists today: Social publishes and reads metrics, can post a first comment and an auto-reply under its own post, and keeps internal review comments (a different thing, `post_comment`). The kernel has `channel_connection`, sealed credentials, `external_call`, `party`, `task`, `activity` and `inbound_message` (email, a record of arrival only). There is no Service module and no store for platform comments or messages. So this design adds the store, and the screens come after it.

## 3. Layout: one app, Ludy Engage

> **Update 2026-11-02 (CV3, #2306):** the owner settled the placement: one app, the existing Social app with no rename, with one Inbox group (Hộp thư) that lists comments and private messages together. See "CV3 as built" in section 13; the text below is the 2026-11-01 analysis.

**Reference (the owner, 2026-11-01, Hootsuite).** One left rail where each product is its own item: Perch (publishing: Plan, Analytics, Ads, Boost), Nest (social inbox and customer care: Conversations with New and Pending, Resolved, Search, Streams, DM automations), and others. Nest takes comments as well as DMs.

**How Ludy's shell works today.** The shell builds its apps, groups and items from each module's entry in `MODULE_SCREENS` (`packages/kernel-contract/src/screens.ts`). A nav item declares `{ app, group, path, label }` (`NavDeclaration` in `manifest.ts`), `apps.ts` lists an app with its groups, and `installedItems` drops every group whose module the deployment does not run (`CROVE_MODULES`); an app with no group left disappears. The rail has four apps today: home, accounting, social, crm. So "an app with groups, each group shown only when its module is enabled" is how the shell already works, and a module can contribute a group to an app another module also contributes to. (This answers the open point in [ads.md](ads.md): a module's nav can sit inside another module's app, no shell change is needed; only a new app id is, if the app is renamed.)

The comparison as first put on 2026-11-01, kept as the record of the options:

## 4. Comments in the inbox, and the bridges

**Decided (owner, 2026-11-03): yes, a comment is a conversation like any other and shows in Hộp thư.** A care person gets one list for everything a customer wrote, on any channel. The post page's Bình luận tab shows the same thread under its post: one store, one unread count, two doors to the same conversation. Three connections link the pieces:

1. **Reply privately.** On a comment, the reply box offers "reply privately" (Meta allows one private message to the commenter within 7 days of the comment [C]; Instagram likewise [C]). It creates a **direct conversation** linked to the comment thread (`parent_conversation_id`). Both threads are in Hộp thư and each keeps a link to the other.
2. **Follow-up work is a task.** From any conversation, "Create task" makes a kernel `task` (a title, an assignee, a due date) that links back to the conversation, and the conversation lists its open tasks. The conversation stays New, Pending or Resolved on its own and the task is ordinary work in the org's task list; how the link is stored (a reference column or a `record_link`) is the build issue's call.
3. **The contact.** Every thread links to the same party, so the contact panel shows a person's comments, messages and emails together for a user who can read them.

An org that has the inbox but not the posting side still sees the comments of the Pages it connects, in Hộp thư, with no post page to open them from. Q2 is settled by the decision above (the options stay in section 16 as the record).

## 5. The core

### 5.1 Where it lives: the kernel

- **Recommendation: kernel tables** (`crove_kernel.conversation` and siblings), next to `channel_connection`, `party`, `task` and `activity`, which the kernel plan already says every module shares. The kernel plan's rule for shared services is the reason: Social and Service must each run without the other, so neither can own the store, and a third module both depend on would force Service to be installed to read a comment.
- **What it costs.** More kernel surface (migrations, protected paths, a security review per PR), personal data in the kernel schema (handled by the org purge, section 10), and a kernel command set that the manifests of both modules reuse. A module-owned store would be less kernel work but would make one module depend on the other.
- **Alternative: a module `conversation`** (schema `crove_conversation`) that Social and Service depend on. Rejected as the default: ADR-0041 explains.

### 5.2 Tables (`crove_kernel`)

Every table has `org_id`, `PRIMARY KEY (org_id, id)`, RLS via `apply_tenant_rls`, composite foreign keys led by `org_id`, `numeric` money (none here), `timestamptz`. Rows are per legal entity where the connection is (a member limited to one entity reads only that entity's conversations).

| Table | Key columns |
|---|---|
| `conversation` | `channel_connection_id`, `kind` (`comment_thread`, `direct`, `email`), `external_thread_id` (the platform's thread, the top comment's id, or an email thread's root Message-ID), `external_post_id` (comment threads: the platform's post or media id; Social joins it to its own `post_variant` on `(channel_connection_id, external_post_id)`, so there is no foreign key across schemas), `parent_conversation_id` (a private reply points at its comment thread), `contact_identity_id`, `subject` (email only: the line the sender wrote, shown in the thread header), `party_id` (set only by a person, section 5.4), `status` (`new`, `pending`, `resolved`), `assignee_user_id`, `last_inbound_at`, `last_message_at`, `reply_window_until` (when the platform's free-reply window closes, from the last inbound message), `opened_at`, `resolved_at`, `resolved_by` |
| `conversation_message` | `conversation_id`, `direction` (`in`, `out`), `external_message_id` (unique per `(org_id, channel_connection_id)`), `sent_at` (the platform's time; ordering uses it), `received_at`, `author_kind` (`contact`, `member`, `agent`, `system`), `author_user_id`, `body`, `attachments` (file ids), `email_refs` (email only: the Message-IDs named by In-Reply-To and References, ids and no text, section 6.7), `send_state` (`queued`, `sent`, `failed`, `unknown`, for `out`), `hidden_at` and `hidden_by` (a comment hidden on the platform), `platform_removed_at` (the author or platform removed it; the body is blanked, section 10), `tainted` (always true for `in`) |
| `conversation_identity` | `provider`, `external_user_id` (a page-scoped id on Meta, an OA-scoped id on Zalo, the lower-cased address of an email sender), `display_name`, `avatar_file_id`, `party_id`, `consent`, unique per `(org_id, provider, external_user_id)`; **every column that names a person is classed `pii`** in the manifest |
| `conversation_note` | `conversation_id`, `author_user_id`, `body`, `created_at`: an internal note, never sent |
| `saved_reply` | `title`, `body`, `shortcut`, `created_by`, `channel_kinds` |
| `conversation_draft` | `conversation_id`, `basis_seq` (the last message when it was asked for), `source` (`auto`, `asked`), `state` (`requested`, `live`, `used`, `discarded`, `stale`), `body` (only while live), `tainted` (always true), the lengths of a used draft (`draft_length`, `final_length`, `edit_size`): the draft of a reply, **written by the system context** (a worker job), never by an org-chart agent; at most one draft in play per conversation (see "CV8 as built") |
| `conversation_read` | `conversation_id`, `user_id`, `last_read_message_id` |
| `conversation_composing` | `conversation_id`, `user_id`, `expires_at`: the "you are replying" marker, pruned by a sweep (section 5.5) |
| `member_availability` | `user_id`, `status` (`available`, `away`), `until` |

Assignment, status changes and resolves also write an `activity` row (ids only, no text, section 9).

### 5.3 Statuses

- **New**: the last message is inbound and nobody has replied since. **Pending**: a person replied and the contact has not answered. **Resolved**: closed by a person (comment threads need a person too). A new inbound message on a resolved conversation reopens it as New. These are the states of the Crisp and Intercom inbox (and Hootsuite Nest's): the tabs only say whose turn it is, and there is no due time and no escalation. An email thread works the same way: a new email on a resolved thread reopens it as New.
- A comment thread is one conversation per **top-level comment** (its replies are its messages), so a busy post does not become one giant thread.

### 5.4 Contact

- The identity is created on first contact (a platform id, and the name and picture the platform gives). **A party is never created automatically**: a person links the conversation to an existing contact or creates one ("Link to contact", "Create contact"), because a comment is not consent to be in a CRM. The contact panel shows the party (name, phone, email, from the kernel `party`, which CRM and Finance share), the identity, and the other conversations of that identity.
- **Names may be missing.** Meta gives a page-scoped id in messages; the profile name and picture need the Business Asset User Profile Access feature, which needs Advanced Access and review [C], and a phone-number Messenger account returns error 2018218 [C]. Comment webhooks list `from.name` in their fields [C] but whether it is filled is [U]. The screen shows "Facebook user ·1234" when there is no name. Zalo gives `display_name` and `avatar` on a user detail call [S] and the phone only if the user shared it [S].

### 5.5 Collision, assignment, availability

The Nest references (a "You are replying" marker, an availability switch, assignment) become:
1. **Assignment is the soft lock.** Opening a New conversation and starting a reply assigns it to the replier unless it is already assigned to someone else (then a banner names them).
2. **Composing marker.** While a person types, an R1 command `kernel.conversation.composing` (at most one per 20 seconds per conversation) upserts `conversation_composing` with a 60-second expiry; the stream drops an invalidation hint to the others who can read the conversation, and the list shows "Linh is replying". A sweep prunes expired rows.
3. **A hard check on send.** `kernel.conversation.reply` carries the id of the last message the sender saw. If a newer message (inbound, or another person's reply, including one sent from the platform's own inbox and seen as an echo) exists, it refuses with `CONVERSATION_MOVED`; the screen shows the new messages and asks again. This is the guarantee; the marker only prevents most clashes.
4. **Availability** is a per-member switch, shown in Hộp thư and used to decide whom to notify about an unassigned New conversation (the assignee if there is one, else the available members who can read it, else owners and admins). No automatic round-robin in v1.

### 5.6 Replies, drafts, saved replies

- A person writes, or picks a saved reply (placeholders for the contact's name only), or takes an AI draft and edits it; **a person presses Send**. The command is R2 (visible to customers); for a user it runs on the UI confirmation (the Send button) per the actor table in the kernel plan, with no approval step in v1 (Q4 is whether members' comment replies need one).
- An AI draft is written by the **system context**, not by an agent: a worker job reads the end of the thread (tainted external text), calls the model in `packages/agent` and records the draft through a system command into `conversation_draft` (always tainted, text only while live). A person asks ("Soạn nháp") or the relay asks for a new conversation, and a person reads the draft, edits it and sends it: no send tool exists for it, and an agent context stays refused everywhere (decision on #2310: no change to ADR-0029's allowlist, no invariant edit). No auto-reply to messages in v1.
- An agent never sends, hides or resolves. The draft is text only.
- **Intent sorting and the contact match** (the same AI coworker, behind the roster map; built under [#2306](https://github.com/DOS/Ludy/issues/2306), CV3, for intent sorting and reply drafts and [#2438](https://github.com/DOS/Ludy/issues/2438) for the contact and company match, not under a build item of this plan). As built in the last part of CV3 ("CV3 as built, second part" in section 13; the issue asks for the suggestions to be computed on read, never stored as truth): an **intent** from five labels (question, order or price, complaint, invoice request, spam) is suggested for a New conversation from the customer's words and shown as a suggestion in the list and the thread, and for a thread not yet linked the **likely contacts** are listed in the contact panel (the contact with the phone number the customer wrote first, deterministic, then contacts with a similar name, which the model only ranks), each with a "Link" button that a person presses. Only a person's applied label is stored (one column on the conversation, written by a command that a trigger limits to a person). Both suggestions are tainted and never actions: nothing is linked, assigned, answered or resolved by them, and a person can ignore or change them. They run behind the AI budget meter like the draft (`metered('conversation.intent' | 'conversation.contact_rank', ...)` under `withAiScope`) and behind the org's switch for AI on its customers' messages. With AI off, spent or failing the suggestions are absent and the inbox works as before.

## 6. How comments and messages arrive

### 6.1 One intake path

- A **public webhook route** per provider family, `POST /api/channels/:provider/webhook` (and, for Meta, the `GET` handshake `hub.mode=subscribe`, `hub.challenge`, `hub.verify_token` [C]), on the reasoned public route list (ADR-0028 enumeration, `route-coverage.test.ts`). It verifies the signature with the deployment's secret, refuses a body over a size limit, and **only parks** the verified body in `crove_kernel.conversation_inbound_pending` (a definer for the API login) before answering 200. As built in CV1 (#2303), it is a parking table and not a pg-boss queue: see the next bullet. It writes no tenant row and does no network call. Meta wants 200 within 5 seconds [C]; Zalo within 2 to 5 seconds [S] and does not retry on an error status [S].
- **Signatures.** Meta: `X-Hub-Signature-256`, HMAC-SHA256 of the payload with the app secret; Meta signs an escaped-Unicode form of the payload, so the check hashes that, not the raw bytes [C]. Zalo: `X-ZEvent-Signature: mac=<hex>` where the hex is `sha256(appId + body + timestamp + oaSecretKey)`; the middle input is ambiguous across sources and the key is the OA secret key, not the app secret [S, [U] until tested on a real event].
- A **worker job** `conversations.ingest` takes the body, finds the **connections from the platform's own account id** (a Page id, an Instagram account id, an OA id) with a definer for the worker login, and for each active connection of an active org runs `kernel.conversation.ingest` (system, through `Runtime.execute()`, R1). **The org comes from the stored connection, never from the notice** (the rule of [ADR-0039](../adr/0039-bank-feed-connectors.md)), so a forged notice cannot write to an org that has no such connection, and the HMAC stops a forged one for a real connection.
- **The body is stored from the webhook**, not re-read, with a difference from ADR-0039 that the data forces: Meta's webhooks have no history API and retry only for 36 hours [C], the Conversations API reads at most the 20 most recent messages [C], and Zalo does not retry on error [S]. So the signed event is the record; the polling job (6.3) is the safety net.
- **Idempotent and unordered.** `UNIQUE (org_id, channel_connection_id, external_message_id)`; a replay is a no-op. Meta does not guarantee order and batches up to 1000 updates [C]; Zalo may resend after an outage with the original timestamp [S]. Order by `sent_at`, store `received_at`.
- **The parked body is personal data**, so it is held only until it is stored, and never in a job. As built in CV1 (#2303) the intake follows ADR-0039's pattern of a pending table and a definer, not a pg-boss queue, because pg-boss keeps job data in its history and copies a failed job's data to the dead letter queue: the worker claims a notice under a lease (`conversation_inbound_claim`), stores its events with `kernel.conversation.ingest`, and deletes it (`conversation_inbound_done`) at once; an unreadable notice is deleted, a notice claimed five times without finishing is deleted with its body, and a notice still waiting after seven days is deleted too (a worker down that long would otherwise hold third parties' bodies indefinitely); a drop the database makes (a poison or an expired notice) is logged and alerted by id and count only, never by text; an unreadable one is logged by id only. The reply and reconcile jobs are pg-boss queues with ids only in their data and declare the platform hosts as their job egress (#1653).

### 6.3 Reconcile (the safety net)

A `conversations.reconcile` job per connection: (1) re-subscribe the Page (an idempotent `POST subscribed_apps`; Meta unsubscribes an app after an hour of failed deliveries on Messenger [C]); (2) read comments of recent posts (hourly for posts under 7 days old, daily to 30 days) and store any not seen; (3) read the 20 most recent messages of recent conversations [C] and store any not seen. It runs within the Page-call budget above. A gap longer than the platforms' own memory cannot be recovered, and the screen says "events missed between X and Y" when a re-subscription had to be made.

### 6.7 Email (a conversation channel; built in #2439, as built below)

Customer email joins the same model: a conversation of kind `email`, shown as a chat thread in Hộp thư and answered in the same box. CV10 (section 13) is built in #2439; the notes marked **As built** say where it differs from the design.

- **Intake.** It reuses the machinery of the inbound mail path (a shared secret, a size limit, MIME parsing in the sandboxed `crove-extract`, the org found from a stored address and never from a header, address rotation #37) with a **conversation address of its own per org**, separate from the document address, so a customer's mail is never read by the invoice extractor [U]. Parsing hands the message to a worker job that runs `kernel.conversation.ingest` for the org that owns the address. Whether a customer's mailbox is forwarded to that address or read from a connected mailbox (IMAP, Gmail, Microsoft 365 through the channel layer) is the build issue's first question [U]; forwarding comes first because the inbound path exists.
  **As built (#2439):** forwarding only. The conversation address is its own random token on the tenant (`conversation_token`), apart from the document address, as designed: publishing it exposes nothing of the invoice reader, rotating it breaks no supplier, and it has its own daily budget (`ORG_LIMIT_CONVERSATION_EMAILS_PER_DAY`, the metric `conversation_emails`). It is made when an owner or admin first opens Hộp thư (`kernel.conversation.address_issue`, so an org that never uses email has none), shown there with a copy button and, for the owner, a "change address" button (`kernel.conversation.address_rotate`, R3: the old token is tombstoned in the same append-only ledger as the document tokens, never issued again, and mail to it is dropped; the same rate limit as the document address, counted apart). A worker finds the org with `org_by_conversation_token`, and a mail to this address never reaches the invoice reader. Only the parser fields the intake needs are read (sender, subject, Message-ID, In-Reply-To, References, Auto-Submitted and list headers, the Authentication-Results headers, the text).
- **Sender (as built).** The sender must be exactly one mailbox (`isMailAddress`: no list, display name, quoted local part or comment), checked at intake and again for every recipient of every mail (`createMailMessage`), since a mail library reads `<a@x,b@y>` as one address and a mailer as two. Nobody is trusted to be who From says: the worker believes the topmost Authentication-Results header written by its own server (`INBOUND_AUTHSERV_ID`, unset means nobody is verified) with `dmarc=pass`, or `dkim=pass` for a signing domain the From domain is or sits under. An unverified mail never joins a thread by its References, opens a thread with no contact link inherited from the customer's other threads, is marked "sender not verified" in the list and header, and gets no contact suggestion by address; a verified mail from the same address clears the mark. The address always shows beside the name the sender chose.
- **Threading.** A message joins the conversation that holds any Message-ID named in its `In-Reply-To` or `References` headers; with none it opens a new conversation whose `external_thread_id` is its own Message-ID. A References id is looked up only inside the same org and the same email connection, so a forged `In-Reply-To` that names another org's conversation opens a new one. The Message-ID is `external_message_id`, so a redelivery is a no-op (the same unique key as every channel), and the ids a message names are kept in `email_refs` (ids only). A subject match with no header link does not merge threads in v1, because a wrong merge shows one customer another's mail [U].
- **Display.** A message shows as a chat bubble: the new text on top, the quoted history and the signature folded behind "show quoted text". The stored body is the whole text (plain text, or HTML reduced to text; HTML is never rendered), so the trimming is a display rule and a wrong trim loses nothing.
- **Attachments.** Images go through the conversation attachment store ("CV5c as built": a row bound to its message, a size cap, the type read from the bytes, never rendered as HTML), taken from the parsed mail instead of fetched from a platform address. Other types show their name, size and type with no bytes in v1; whether PDFs and office files are stored under the same rules is the build issue's call [U].
  **As built (#2439):** no bytes at all yet: a mail with attachments shows a one-line count note in the body, and storing images from mail is a follow-up.
- **Replies.** `kernel.conversation.reply` on an `email` thread queues a mail through the kernel mail pipeline ([ADR-0034](../adr/0034-outbound-email.md): the `mail_message` row before the provider call, the suppression check, one send per mail, the `external_call` key `mail:<message>:<attempt>`) on the transactional stream, with `In-Reply-To` and `References` set so the customer's client threads it. Email has no reply window and no private-reply limit. A bounce or a complaint event marks the message `failed`, and the suppression list applies as for every sender. The send is R2, a person presses Send, and an agent never sends.
  **As built (#2439):** the reply is a `kernel.conversation_reply` template mail whose Reply-To is the conversation address, so the customer's answer comes back to the inbox; the mail queue settles the message `sent`, `failed` (provider failure, suppression, hard bounce) or `unknown` (the outcome is not known, shown with "was sent" only: an unknown email send is never sent again by a button). Erasure, the retention purge and the Meta deletion callback null the reply mail's `to_address`, `subject` and `payload` as they delete the thread (the keyed hash stays, so a bounce still suppresses the address); a mail already handed to the mailer cannot be recalled. The mail retention clears fields of the other mails by its own schedule and deletes no row.
- **Loops and noise.** A message marked auto-submitted or bulk, or sent from the org's own address, opens no conversation and gets no reply; a conversation never replies by itself (no auto-send, section 5.6).
- **Permissions and personal data.** The sender's address is the identity (`external_user_id`, class `pii`). The body and attachments follow section 10 (retention, `erase_contact`, export, purge), and an `email` thread is read, answered and erased by the same people as a direct message (section 8): mail to a business is private.

## 7. How replies go out

- `kernel.conversation.reply` writes a `conversation_message` (`out`, `send_state` queued) and an `external_call` row with the deterministic key `conversation.reply:<message>:<attempt>`, `started`, committed **before** the call, exactly as the publish pipeline does ([social.md](social.md) section 3.4). The worker sends; the adapter answers `accepted` (the platform's message id), `rejected` (typed: `token_expired`, `permission`, `bad_request`, `rate_limited` with a retry time, `transient`) or `unknown`.
- **Never a blind retry**: none of these send calls has an idempotency key, so a send is `reconcile_only`. An `unknown` outcome becomes `unknown` on the message and a reconcile reads the thread: for Messenger the `message_echoes` event for the Page carries the `mid` of what the Page sent [C]; for comments the comment list of the post; for Zalo the `oa_send_*` echo or the conversation history [S]. A person can resolve it by hand.
- **Window check before the send.** `reply_window_until` is computed from `last_inbound_at` (24 hours on Meta, 48 on Zalo [S]). Inside it, send. On Meta past it, only a person's reply with the HUMAN_AGENT tag up to 7 days [C, [U] for approval]; past that, the Send button is disabled with the reason. On Zalo past 48 hours a message may be charged against the OA's quota [S]; v1 refuses it with a message and does not send paid messages.
- **Rate limits and typed errors** as section 6.2 and 6.5; the channel layer's `rate_limited` result delays the job to the retry time. A `token_expired` result sets the connection `expired`, pauses sending, and alerts, as for publishing; inbound events already stored are not lost.
- **Comment replies** have the same pipeline with the comment id as the target; **hide and unhide** are R2 commands with the same pipeline, reversible; **delete** is out of v1.
- **Email replies** use the kernel mail pipeline instead of a platform call (section 6.7); the rest, a person's Send and the check for a newer message, is the same.

## 8. Permissions

Kind decides who may read, because a public comment and a private message are different in what they expose.

| Action | Comment thread | Direct message or email |
|---|---|---|
| Read | owner, admin, member and viewer who may read the channel (the existing per-channel limit applies) | owner, admin, and members holding the `desk.agent` role (a new permission `conversation.direct.read`); the per-channel limit applies; viewers never |
| Reply, hide, resolve, assign, write a note | owner, admin, member who may read it (R2, user confirmation) | owner, admin, `desk.agent` role holders |
| Use an AI draft | those who may reply | those who may reply |
| Link or create a contact | those who may reply, and may edit parties | the same |
| Erase a contact's data (section 10) | owner, admin, R3 | owner, admin, R3 |
| Retention and saved-reply settings, connect the inbox scopes | owner, admin | owner, admin |

- **Email follows the direct message rule.** An `email` conversation is read, answered and erased by the same people as a direct message, and the database enforces it with the same restrictive policy (`kind <> 'comment_thread'`).
- **Roles.** `desk.agent` exists in `BUILTIN_ROLE_KEYS` and grants nothing yet; it gains the direct-message permissions. Social has no role key yet and uses the member rule it has today. The permissions are declared in the manifests, checked by `manifest-permissions.test.ts`, and enforced by the database (a restrictive policy on `kind = 'direct'` that asks the same role-assignment check the finance permissions use), not only in the screen.
- **The per-channel limit moves to the kernel.** Social's `channel_access` (one row per member and channel, #1081) is in `crove_social`, which the kernel cannot read, so the core cannot apply it. A build step copies it to `crove_kernel.channel_access` and Social reads it from there (a one-time migration, no behaviour change). Without it a freelancer limited to one client's channels would read every channel's messages.
- **Customer care roles for a freelancer or an agency:** the client-side reading of conversations is out of v1.

## 9. Tenancy, writes, audit, agents

- **One write path.** Every write is a command through `Runtime.execute()`: `kernel.conversation.ingest` (system, worker only), `reply`, `hide`, `assign`, `resolve`, `reopen`, `link_contact`, `note`, `follow_up` (R1, creates the linked task), `composing`, `erase_contact`, `purge_expired`, `set_availability`, plus the draft write by an agent. The roles' direct SQL writes are zero.
- **No text in audit rows or activity.** Audit rows and the WORM stream cannot be erased later, so a command's audit target is the conversation and message ids and lengths, never a body or a name (the rule of ADR-0037). Activity rows carry ids only. Logs carry no bodies.
- **Taint.** Every inbound message and comment is tainted external text (`source_trust external`). An agent reading it gets the tainted-run rules: R1 writes go to review, send tools are hidden, and its only conversation write is a draft (section 5.6).
- **Agent allowlist.** Unchanged (#2310). A draft is written by the system context, so `conversation_draft` is not added to the tables agent contexts may write: `apply_tenant_rls` installs the agent write guard on it like every new tenant table, and `conversation_access` is false for an agent.
- **Invariants to add** (each a test in the suite, written by the build issue that adds the table): cross-org and cross-entity RLS for every table; a direct message unreadable by a member without the permission and by a limited-channel member; a `conversation_message` body never in audit, activity, events or logs; ingestion writes only through the system command and only for a connection that exists; webhook routes on the public list and each verifying its signature before any enqueue; a notice cannot name an org.
- **Manifest classes** (#1599): body, display name, avatar and external user id are `pii`; phone and email on the party stay `contact`; the build issue checks the P04 rule for any command that writes a `contact` field.
- **Job egress** (#1653): `conversations.reply` and `conversations.reconcile` declare the platform hosts; the commands that feed them declare an external effect.

## 10. Personal data

Comments and messages are **personal data of third parties** under the PDPL (Law 91/2025, Decree 356/2025, as ADR-0037 cites them), and the org is the controller.

- **Retention.** An org setting, default **24 months after a conversation's last message** (Q5), after which a daily job deletes it (`kernel.conversation.purge_expired`, system), with the choices 6, 12, 24 months and "keep until deleted by a person". Notes go with their conversation.
- **Erasure on request.** `kernel.conversation.erase_contact` (R3, owner or admin) deletes an identity's messages, notes, drafts and attachments across both kinds, anonymizes the party link, and writes an audit row with ids only. It is the path a person's PDPL request takes, from the contact panel.
- **Platform removal.** When the platform tells us a comment or message was removed (a `remove` or `delete` verb on a Page comment [C]; Zalo's echo events [S]), the body is blanked and `platform_removed_at` set. Meta's terms require deleting platform data when the user asks or no longer has an account [C].
- **Meta's data deletion callback** (HTTPS `signed_request`, answered with a `url` and a `confirmation_code` [C]) is about the **app's users**, which here means the Ludy member who connected a Page, not the customers who messaged it. Receiving it disconnects that member's connections and **deletes the conversation data read through them** unless the org's owner keeps it by taking over the connection first. This is the cautious reading of Meta's rule; whether a business's own customer-care records fall under it is [U] and a legal read (the lawyer, through the owner) before Ludy Cloud serves other businesses. The same callback serves Ads ([ads.md](ads.md) section 9).
- **Disconnect** keeps the stored conversations by default as the org's service history, with a clear "delete too" choice, as for Ads (Q4 there).
- **Attachments.** Meta's attachment URLs and Zalo's media expire (Zalo keeps media 7 days [S]), so a customer's image must be downloaded when the message arrives. v1 stores **images up to a size cap** as files bound to their message (`crove_kernel.conversation_attachment`, not a `crove_kernel.file` row: see "CV5c as built"), fetched through the egress guard, never rendered as HTML and never parsed by the extraction pipeline; videos, audio and files show "open on the platform" until a later slice. They are deleted with their message.
- **What is not stored:** the commenter's or sender's profile data beyond the name, picture and id the platform gives; no phone number unless the contact shared it and a person saved it on the party.
- **Export and purge:** conversations are tenant data, so the org export includes them (the export notes they hold personal data of third parties) and the org purge deletes them (a definer for the worker, like the other purges). The export lists a customer's images as rows of metadata (type, size, hash, time) and never copies their bytes; see "CV5c as built".

The summary of a thread carries `composing` (the other members typing now), the detail carries `others` (the customer's other threads the reader may read, no text), the list takes `q`.
- **The marker is read with the screens' six-second poll, not through a stream hint.** A hint reaches every member of the org today; a hint only readers of a thread get needs its own audience rule and invariant, so it is a follow-up (P3). A sweep (`conversation_composing_sweep`, worker login, run by the relay) drops markers five minutes after they expired.
- **A new conversation is told to** the assignee when there is one and they can read it, else the available members who can read it, else owners and admins (the notification `kernel.conversation_new`, ids only, one per time a thread becomes New: the first message, or a customer writing again after it was answered or resolved). Who can read it is the definer `conversation_readers` (worker login, in an org's system context), the same rule as `conversation_access`, never a viewer.
- **Home.** `kernel.conversation.today` (R0, TodayItem app `conversations`): the conversations that wait for the member by their own read rule (New, unassigned or assigned to them; nothing for a viewer or for a thread they may not read), one count item and the oldest five with the end of the reply window as `dueAt`, `href` inside the org (`/social/inbox?status=new`, `/social/inbox?c=<id>`), no text and no name.
- **Copy.** The reply-draft copy that named Vy now names no AI coworker (the owner revised the coworker names on 2026-11-02; the name comes back through the roster placeholder of the app's copy when it lands, in one place).
- **Not in this part:** the intent sorting, the applied label and its filter and the contact match (see "CV3 as built, second part"), and the stream hint for the marker.

### CV3 as built, second part (#2306): the intent and the contact match

The last part of the issue, which closes it. A person decides, the AI coworker only suggests, and every call goes through the AI budget meter (#2382).
- **The label is the only thing stored.** `crove_kernel.conversation.intent` (one of `question`, `order_or_price`, `complaint`, `invoice_request`, `spam`, or null) with `intent_set_by` and `intent_set_at`, written by the command `kernel.conversation.set_intent` (R1, owners, admins and members, the thread's own read and write rule applies; a person applies, changes or clears it with one click). The trigger `conversation_intent_guard` refuses every writer but a person naming themselves: a thread is born with no label, the system and any other actor never label one, a cleared label names nobody, and a label and its time come together (a CHECK). Nothing is routed, assigned, resolved or hidden by a label. The audit row holds the label (one of five words) and the one before it, never a text. The list filters by it (`intent=<label>` or `none`).
- **The suggestion is a read, never stored.** The API procedure `conversations.suggestIntents` reads, in the member's own transaction, the customer's last three messages of each thread the member may read (`kernel.conversation.intent_source`, cut to 300 characters, at most ten threads), then calls `InboxReader.intents` outside any transaction. It is not a command, so no agent or system actor is involved, and it writes no row and no audit row. The answer is checked after the call: one of the five labels, for a thread that was asked about, once; "other", an unknown word and any other number give no suggestion. It is shown in the proposal hue as "Gợi ý: …", and only a click applies it. The New list may be ordered by what shows (a person's label, else the suggestion), a sort of the page on screen, not a write. What the model has said for a thread's newest message is kept for an hour in the API process's memory (a convenience so the six-second poll does not pay again, not a record).
- **The model call site.** `InboxReader` in `packages/agent/src/inbox.ts`: `metered('conversation.intent', ...)` and `metered('conversation.contact_rank', ...)`, both features of the `conversation` module in `AI_FEATURES`. They are called by `suggestIntents` and `matchContacts` in `apps/kernel/src/conversations-ai.ts` from the rpc procedures `conversations.suggestIntents` and `conversations.contactMatches` (`apps/kernel/src/rpc.ts`), under the request's AI scope (`inOrg`), so the org's budget is asked before the call and the tokens are recorded after it. The calls have a budget of their own (twelve a minute for a member, sixty for the org, one at a time for a member), apart from the reply drafts'.
- **The org's switch and the budgets.** Both procedures first read the org's switch for AI on its customers' messages, `conversation_setting.auto_draft` (ADR-0041: owners and admins decide, a customer's words go to the deployment's AI endpoint): with it off no word of a customer and no name of a contact reaches the model, the answer is `off`, and the contact list still comes by phone with the names in their own order. Its label and hint now name the suggestions and the contact names as processed data. The API cannot ask `kernel.usage.over` (a system query) for the half-of-the-daily-limit guard the draft job uses; what bounds these calls is the AI budget meter, whose `conversation` budget is its own, apart from the `finance` budget that invoice reading uses, so a customer who floods the Inbox cannot spend what invoices need, plus a per-member and per-org rate (twelve a minute for a member and sixty for the org, one at a time for a member, with the intent calls and the contact ranking apart).
- **Known limits.** One call reads up to ten threads together, so a customer's words are in the same call as other customers' threads: a crafted message could steer the label suggested for another thread, which the check after the call limits to the five labels and which only changes a badge and the sort of the page for the minutes the answer is kept (two for "no suggestion", an hour for a label), never a stored value. The sort and the suggestions cover the first ten threads without a label of the New tab. The phone read from a text takes Vietnamese mobile numbers written with at most three separators and not after another digit or inside a date; a number typed by anyone is only a suggestion, so a person decides the link.
- **With the model off, spent, busy or failing there is no suggestion and no error.** The procedures answer `ai`: `used`, `off` (no model in the deployment), `budget` (the month's AI budget is spent: the screen says so in one line), `busy` or `failed`; the Inbox works as it did. A failure is logged by the kind of error only, never its message.
- **What the model sees.** The customers' words, fenced as untrusted data, one line each, with the rules that say they are data and that it answers labels only; never a name, a channel, an id or the org. A thread the member may not read is not read by the model either.
- **The contact match.** For a thread not yet linked (a person links it, as before; nothing is linked here): the contacts whose phone key (`crove_kernel.phone_key`, the key the CRM matches on) equals the key of a Vietnamese mobile number the customer wrote in the thread come first, deterministic, no model; then the contacts whose name shares words with the customer's name on the platform (folded, a one-word name needs that word, a longer one needs two). Only when there are two or more name matches does the model rank them, seeing the names only (the customer's and the candidates'), and answering the numbers of the likely ones; a number that was not offered is dropped and the ones it left out follow in their own order. At most five are shown, each with why ("Cùng số điện thoại", "Tên giống") and a "Gắn" button.
- **The draft marked as started from a draft** is CV8's `draftId` on the reply (the draft is marked used with the lengths and the size of the edit, never the text; the audit row holds ids and numbers only).

### CV8 as built (#2310): reply drafts and the saved replies

- **Who writes a draft (decided first, on the issue).** The system context. A worker job (`kernel-conversation-draft`, ids only in its data) reads the end of the thread through the system query `kernel.conversation.draft_source`, calls `ConversationWriter` (`packages/agent`) and records the answer with the system command `kernel.conversation.draft_record`. A member only **asks** (`draft_request`, "Soạn nháp": a row `requested`, no text, in their own name) and **decides** (`draft_discard`, or a reply that began from it). So ADR-0029's agent write allowlist is untouched and no invariant changes: an agent context is refused by `agent_write_guard` (installed on every tenant table by `apply_tenant_rls`) and `conversation_access` is false for an agent. The text and the tainted mark are the system's alone, enforced by the trigger `conversation_draft_guard` (a member's insert is a request in their own name with no text; a member's update only decides; a decided draft is final).
- **Tables.** `crove_kernel.conversation_draft` (`ON DELETE CASCADE` from its thread; read through the thread's own rule `conversation_access`; at most one `requested` or `live` draft per conversation by a partial unique index; the text exists only while the draft is live, so a retention purge or an erasure has little of the draft's text to delete; a used draft keeps `draft_length`, `final_length` and `edit_size`, numbers for later learning, never text) and `crove_kernel.saved_reply` (the org's own text: title, body up to 2000 characters, keywords; read by every member, written by owners, admins and members, 200 at most). `conversation_setting.auto_draft` (default on).
- **When a draft is made.** A new conversation (the customer wrote last, the customer has been quiet for 20 seconds, less than 48 hours ago, the message not removed, the platform's reply window open, at most three automatic drafts an hour for one conversation): the relay asks `crove_kernel.conversation_draft_due` (worker login only, every ten seconds, five at a time) and hands each through `draft_enqueue`; or a member presses "Soạn nháp" (refused when the thread cannot be answered). Both emit `kernel.conversation.draft_queued` (ids only) for a queue that declares `provider:ai` egress. A draft goes `stale` and drops its text when a newer message arrives (the customer's, or the org's own from the platform's inbox), when the platform removes the message it answered, when the customer's messages in a thread are erased, and after seven days unopened; a dismissed or failed draft is not made again for the same message (the relay checks `basis_seq`); a request the job never answered is settled after 15 minutes. The seven days and the fifteen minutes are the sweep `crove_kernel.conversation_draft_sweep` (worker login only), which the relay runs in every deployment, with a model or without, so the text of a live draft never outlives its seven days (ADR-0041). With no AI endpoint, a request is settled at once as none (`AI_OFF`) and the reply box says nothing.
- **What the draft says.** In Vietnamese unless the customer's last message is clearly in another language, one to four plain sentences, within the channel's reply limit. It is told never to invent a price, stock, date, time, address, phone number, discount, delivery time, link or promise, never to confirm a figure only the customer wrote, and to write a bracket for a fact it was not given ("[giá]"). The checks after the call do not trust that. A figure must have been stated by the business (its own messages and the org's saved replies); the customer's words give back only small numbers (a quantity, a size, a house number) and a phone number, never a price; figures are compared after NFKC with invisible characters removed and digits split by spaces joined, a digit that is not 0 to 9 refuses the draft, a decimal is never read as a whole figure ("1.5" is not "15"), and an amount with its unit ("150k", "1tr2", "500đ", "10%") or a number in words ("một triệu hai trăm nghìn") must appear whole in what the business stated. A link, a bare web address ("bit.ly/x"), an "hxxp" address or an `@handle` must be in what the business stated. An empty text or one over the limit also makes **no draft** (the code is kept, nothing is cut or repaired). In the prompt each message is one line (a customer cannot fake the business's turn), customer text and the customer's name are fenced as untrusted data, and the model sees only the last 12 messages and at most five saved replies.
- **Saved replies beside the draft.** Matched without the model: a reply scores one for each of its keywords the customer's last message contains as a whole word or phrase, with no case and no diacritics; the best three are offered, the person picks, edits and sends (R2). The matched ones (at most five) are also given to the model as the facts it may reuse.
- **Used and corrected.** A reply that names its draft (`draftId`) marks it used and records only lengths and the size of the edit; a reply that does not name the live draft discards it; a saved reply it names is counted.
- **The org's AI limit and cost.** The model call is rationed by the existing per-minute draft budget of the deployment (`apps/kernel/src/ai-draft-limit.ts`, #386): the API takes a ticket per member and org when a member asks (`RATE_LIMITED` over it), and the worker takes the org's part for every draft (over it the draft is dropped with `RATE_LIMITED` and the person can ask again); this feature has no counter of its own. Before the call the job asks `kernel.usage.over` for `ai_cost` at **half** the org's daily limit (so a customer who writes a lot cannot use up what invoice reading needs): over it there is no draft and no mail (`AI_LIMIT`); the call itself goes through the AI budget meter (#2382: `metered('conversation.draft', ...)` in `ConversationWriter`, the job running under `withAiScope` with the system meter), which refuses it when the org's monthly AI budget is spent (`AI_BUDGET`, no draft, no mail) and records its tokens and cost as a usage row, so the job records none of its own; the half-limit check above reads the same rows. The model call site is `ConversationWriter.draft` in `packages/agent/src/conversation.ts` (its default generator), called by `runConversationDraft` in `apps/kernel/src/conversations-worker.ts`. The org's owners and admins switch drafting of new conversations on or off (`auto_draft_set`): a customer's message goes to the deployment's AI endpoint for it, so the switch is the org's control of that processing (a draft a member asks for is theirs to ask).
- **No text in audit rows, events, jobs or logs.** The audit rows carry draft and conversation ids, lengths and codes; the event and the job carry the draft and conversation ids; a model error (which may quote the prompt) is never logged. Tested.
- **Export and purge.** The export lists both tables (a live draft's text is third-party-derived personal data, like the messages); the org purge deletes both (carried from the newest definition); an erasure or the retention purge deletes drafts with their thread.
- **Not in this slice:** the reply-box part moving into the Hộp thư inbox (CV3 #2306 reuses `DraftPanel`), learning from the recorded edits, a per-channel voice, placeholders in saved replies, drafts for comment threads on Social (the table and the job do not care about the kind; the Social comment inbox shows the same panel).

### CV5c as built (#2357): customers' images

- **The read rule, decided first: a table of its own, not a file.** `crove_kernel.file` is deduplicated by content (`UNIQUE (org_id, sha256)`, blob key `org/<org>/<sha256>`), so two customers who send the same photo, or a member who uploads the same bytes, would share one row and one blob: no row can belong to one message, and deleting one message's file would delete or keep another's bytes. A file row is also reached by the file list, the file route, extraction, evidence holds and the export. So an image is a `crove_kernel.conversation_attachment` row (`ON DELETE CASCADE` from its message, so it goes with erase_contact, purge_expired, erase_connection, the org purge and a platform removal), read through a restrictive policy that asks the message's own rule (`crove_kernel.conversation_access(kind, channel_connection_id)`: channel limit, the direct-message permission, owners and admins) and written, updated and deleted by the system context alone. No kernel file row is ever made, so the generic file list, the file route and the files export cannot see an image by construction. Approved by the Notes & CRM PO and the design owner.
- **Bytes under a key of their own.** `conversation/<org_id>/<random 64 hex>`, reserved when the row is made, never content addressed and never shared, so deleting a row deletes exactly its bytes. The row is made first (`pending`, with the platform's address) and the blob written after (`stored`, the address cleared), so a row always names its blob and no blob is written that no row names.
- **Where the address lives.** On the pending row (`source_url`, personal data, manifest class `pii`), cleared the moment the bytes are stored, and by deleting the row at six days (Zalo keeps media for seven [S]) or after five tries. It never reaches a job (ids only), an event, an audit row or a log; the export omits the column.
- **The fetch.** The relay asks `crove_kernel.conversation_attachment_due` (worker login only) and hands each row through `kernel.conversation.attachment_request` (R2, external, emits `attachment_queued`, ids only) to a job on the reply queue. The job reads the address with the system query `attachment_source`, fetches through the egress guard (`guardedFetch` with a host allowlist asked on every redirect hop, a 5 MiB cap, 20 seconds, no compression), reads the type from the first bytes (JPEG, PNG, WebP or GIF; never SVG, never what the host declared) and records the result (`attachment_record`). Zalo's media hosts are `zdn.vn`, `zadn.vn`, `zalo.me`, `zaloapp.com` and `zalocdn.com` [S, U until live-check row 18], overridable with `CHANNEL_ZALO_MEDIA_HOSTS`. A host that is not allowed, a bad type, a body over the cap or a gone address drops the image for good (the message keeps its placeholder); a timeout or a server error asks again later. Only `user_send_image` carries images; other media stay placeholders.
- **The bytes route.** `GET /api/conversations/attachments/:id`, authenticated like every route. It reads the row through the caller's own policy (`kernel.conversation.attachment_get`), so a member who may not read the thread gets the same 404 as an id that does not exist, and no byte. It serves images only: the type is read from the bytes again at serve time, anything else is 404, with `X-Content-Type-Options: nosniff`, `Content-Security-Policy: default-src 'none'; sandbox`, `Content-Disposition: inline; filename="image.<ext>"` and `Cache-Control: private, no-store`. The screen draws it from an object URL in an `<img>`, never as HTML, with the message's placeholder text as the fallback.
- **Deleting the blobs.** A trigger queues the key of every deleted row in `crove_kernel.conversation_blob_doomed`, in the same transaction as the delete, so every path that deletes a row (the cascade of a message, a thread, a customer, a connection, the org purge, a platform removal, the expiry) queues its blob; the worker's relay deletes them from the store (`sweepConversationBlobs`, at least a minute apart) and tells the database. A stored image's key is due at once; a pending image's after an hour, so a fetch in flight when its row went cannot leave an object nobody names (this is also the backstop for a crash between the write and the record). The org purge returns the keys of stored images with the files' and deletes them before it commits, as for files. Nothing but ids and counts is logged.
- **Export:** a customer's images appear in the org export as rows of metadata only (`kernel.conversation_attachment`: type, size, hash, size in pixels, times, never the address); the bytes stay in the store under `conversation/<org_id>/` and are not copied, because the export would hold third-party images beyond the org's own deletion path. The export's own note about personal data of third parties covers the rows. Tested.
- **Not in this slice:** videos, audio, files and stickers (placeholders), images on outbound messages, a thumbnail, an image on a Facebook or Instagram comment (the table and the route do not care about the provider; each connector adds its own parser). Runbook: [conversations-data-rights.md](../runbooks/conversations-data-rights.md); the Zalo shapes are live-check rows 18 to 20.

Each question: what is decided, why it matters, the options, my recommendation, and what happens without an answer. One at a time, the first one first.

**Q1. Layout: one rail app with groups, or separate rail items?** (Who acts: JOY, with the Frontend line for the name.) It decides how the app looks to every customer and one rename in the shell.
- **A. One rail app with three groups: posting, inbox, ads, each shown only when its module is enabled (recommended).** The shell already works this way; short rail for an SME; Hootsuite itself holds Ads inside its publishing product.
- B. Separate rail items for Social, Service and Ads, like Hootsuite's rail. Matches the screenshot, but a longer rail and a context switch between a comment and the private reply it started.

Without an answer: A, with the working name "Kênh".
**Answered** (JOY, 2026-11-01): A. The groups are those of section 3.

**Q2. Should a comment also appear in the Service inbox?** (Who acts: JOY.) It decides whether care teams see comments.
- **A. No: comments only in Social, with "reply privately" and "send to Service" bridges (recommended).** Your decision as written; one place per item.
- B. Also show comments in Service as a filter, only when Social is not enabled. A Service-only org then sees comments; a small screen change.
- C. Always show comments in both. Two unread counts and two people answering the same comment.

Without an answer: A.
**Answered, differently** (JOY, 2026-11-03): comments show in Hộp thư, with the post tab as a second door. The options above are the record of the question as put on 2026-11-01.

**Q3. What is the app called?** (Who acts: JOY with the Frontend line.) It decides the rail label and the menus. Options: **Kênh** (recommended, short, "channels"), Ludy Channels, a name per customer type. Without an answer: "Kênh" as a working name, changeable later because it is only a label. **Answered** (JOY, 2026-11-02): **Ludy Engage**.

**Q4. Do members' public replies to comments need approval?** (Who acts: JOY.) It decides whether replying in the org's name is as fast as in Hootsuite or as controlled as publishing.
- **A. No approval in v1: a member who may read the channel can reply; an AI draft always needs a person to press Send (recommended).** Speed matters in care; owners can limit a freelancer to some channels already.
- B. Replies by members need an owner or admin approval, like posts. Safer for agencies, slow for one-person companies.

Without an answer: A.

**Q5. How long are comments and messages kept?** (Who acts: JOY, a lawyer for the legal side.) It decides storage and what the privacy page promises.
- **A. 24 months after the last message, the owner can choose 6, 12, 24 or keep (recommended).** Long enough for a dispute, short enough to be defensible.
- B. 12 months. Smaller exposure, less history for care.
- C. Keep until deleted by a person. Simplest, the largest pile of third parties' personal data.

Without an answer: A, and a lawyer reads Meta's rule on stored customer messages before Ludy Cloud.

**Q6. Zalo OA messaging needs the customer's paid OA plan with API integration. Accept that?** (Who acts: JOY.) It decides what Ludy tells Zalo customers.
- **A. Yes: show a clear message "your OA plan has no API integration" and document the plan (recommended).** It is Zalo's rule; nothing in Ludy can lift it.
- B. Delay Zalo until its pricing is confirmed on an official page the build issue can read.

Without an answer: A, and the live check confirms the plan names and quotas.

**Q7. In what order do we build?** (Who acts: JOY.) It decides what you can try first.
- **A. Core and comments on the sandbox, Facebook Page comments, the app and the Hộp thư inbox on the sandbox, Messenger, then Zalo, then retention (recommended).** You see comments first; each step runs without any platform review.
- B. Zalo OA messages before Messenger, because Zalo is Vietnam's first channel. Needs the Hộp thư inbox first (CV3), so the first visible result is later.

Without an answer: A.

## 1. The user and the weekly job

The owner of a one-person company or a small team meets every week: with staff on Monday, with a customer to agree a quote, with a supplier on prices and dates. Today what was said lives in someone's head or a phone voice memo nobody replays, and the promises made ("anh gửi báo giá trước thứ Sáu") are forgotten.

After the first slice, that person can:

1. capture the meeting one of three ways: with the Ludy browser extension for an online meeting in Chrome or Edge (Google Meet, or Teams and Zoom opened in the browser), with the microphone in the Ludy app for a meeting in a room, or by uploading a recording;
2. confirm that everyone in the meeting was told it is recorded;
3. get back, a few minutes after the meeting and for their eyes only, a Vietnamese transcript with timestamps (and speakers when the provider gives them), a short summary, the decisions taken, and the action items with who and when, then share it with the attendees in one click;
4. turn the action items into team tasks with one click each, or one click for all the clean ones;
5. keep the video too when they tick it for that meeting;
6. know that the audio is gone after 30 days, a kept video after 7, and the transcript after the period the org chose.

## 3. The scope gate

- **Fit.** An owner or salesperson of a small business meets several times a month, and the weekly job (remember what was agreed and follow it up) is the one this shortens. Enterprise features (a bot in every call, compliance recording, meeting analytics for managers) wait (section 11).
- **Smallest slice.** One person, the defaults, no setup beyond adding the extension once: capture, confirm the notice, read the result, accept the tasks. Three settings only, all needed by law or cost: how long audio, video and transcripts are kept (section 9).
- **AI part, named.** In the meeting screen Vy (`{gm}`) transcribes, summarizes, lists the decisions and drafts the action items, each with a link to the moment in the transcript it comes from. Review stays short: items whose checks pass are accepted in one batch. Model calls go through `metered()` under `withAiScope` with new `AI_FEATURES` keys `meetings.transcribe` and `meetings.summary`. AI output is tainted; a person approves every task. With AI off the module still records, stores, plays back and takes typed notes and tasks.
- **Proportionate security.** Tenancy, approvals and the personal-data rules stay as they are; a meeting gets the narrowest readers that work (section 8), because a recording holds voices of people who are not members.

## 5. The first slice

### 5.1 The flow

1. **New meeting** (in Ludy Notes, Vy's app, as a "Cuộc họp" tab): a title (pre-filled with the date), the language (Vietnamese by default, or English or automatic), and the attendees who are members of the org (optional).
2. **The notice.** Before recording starts or an upload is accepted, the person ticks "Mọi người trong cuộc họp đã được báo là cuộc họp được ghi âm" and picks how they were told (said at the start, written in the invite, agreed in writing). The screen shows a one-sentence notice to read aloud. The server refuses audio for a meeting without this record (section 7).
3. **Capture** one of the three ways of section 5.5, with the "Ghi cả video" checkbox off by default (owner decision of 2026-11-04). A recording is saved as it goes on the person's device, so a closed tab does not lose it, and uploaded when stopped. Only the audio is kept unless the person ticked video for this meeting.
4. **Processing** (a job, section 6): the media sandbox checks and normalizes the audio; the recorder's Vy transcribes it and drafts the summary, decisions and action items. All of it lands in the **recorder's own Vy result**: only the recorder sees it (section 8). The meeting shows "Đang chép lời", then "Vy đã xong, chờ bạn xem" on the recorder's Home.
5. **Review, by the recorder.** The transcript with timestamps; speakers as "Người nói 1, 2..." when the provider separates them, renamed for this meeting; the text can be corrected. Beside it, the summary, the decisions and the action items, each drafted item styled "Đề xuất" with a link to its moment.
6. **Share.** "Chia sẻ với người dự họp" publishes the reviewed result to the meeting's readers in one click. Until then the attendees see the meeting and its audio, not Vy's result.
7. **Tasks.** "Tạo việc" on an item, or "Tạo N việc" for all clean items, creates team tasks (section 5.3): the recorder from the draft, any reader once published.

### 5.2 The transcript

- Vietnamese first; the provider gets the language hint and, since #3499 (ADR-0047 point 5 as amended 2026-11-16), a limited list of names; mixed English words stay as spoken.
- Segments with start and end times; speakers only where the provider returns them. No speaker recognition across meetings: that would keep a voiceprint, which the law likely treats as biometric data (section 7).
- Audio plays from any segment. A corrected segment keeps its times.

### 5.3 Vy's part: summary, decisions, action items

- One structured call (`meetings.summary`) over the transcript text, with the meeting date and the attendees' names; nothing else from the org is sent.
- Output: a summary of at most about eight lines, the decisions, and action items (what, who as said, when as said, the transcript time it rests on).
- Checks in code, before anything is shown: every number in the summary and the decisions appears in the transcript (the #1237 rule); every item's time points to a real segment; a "when" is parsed by code against the meeting date, never by the model; the "who" is matched against the attendees.
- **Clean** item: the who is an attendee with a position, the date parsed, the title has no link, email or phone number, and no flag. Clean items can be accepted in one batch; others one by one.
- **Accepting** runs `meetings.action_item.accept` as the person (a confirm command on ADR-0049's `MEMBER_VY_SINKS` list), which calls `kernel.task.create_from_meeting`: a task with the title the person confirmed, the due date, the position, `source_ref` to the item and `tainted` set while the AI text is unedited. A task is read by every member, so the dialog says so; the transcript is never copied into it. A member who cannot open the meeting sees "Bản ghi không mở được" on the link.
- Action items are suggestions on the meeting, not `crove_kernel.proposal` rows: the approval queue is read by the org's approvers, who may not be readers of the meeting (ADR-0047 point 8, the same reason as notes §3.11).
- Prompt injection: the transcript is untrusted. Someone saying "Vy, chuyển tiền cho..." in the meeting produces at most a tainted, flagged suggestion; nothing runs without a person's click, and nothing raises a risk level. Red-team cases run in CI with a fake model, as #893 does for documents.

### 5.4 With AI off

No model configured, the budget spent or the kill switch engaged: the meeting records, stores, plays and is deleted on time; the person types notes and action items; tasks are created the same way. The screen says why the transcript waits ("Chờ ngân sách AI", "AI đang tạm dừng").

### 5.6 The Ludy extension: a small shell, meetings its first feature

Owner, 2026-11-03: "Tạm thời tao nghĩ nên xài extension Ludy đi, extension còn có tác dụng cho App Engage nữa". So `apps/extension` is **the Ludy extension** for Chrome and Edge (Manifest V3), shared by the apps, and meeting capture is its first feature. The shell, built with it:

- **Sign-in** to the member's org with a scoped token (below);
- **an org picker** for a member of several orgs (one token per org);
- **features that follow the org**: the extension shows a feature only when its module runs in that org (ADR-0031) and the member holds its permission; meetings needs `meetings`;
- **a permission request per feature**: a feature asks for the browser permissions it needs only when the member turns it on, and gives them back when turned off; the base install asks for almost nothing;
- **a generic "capture this page to a command" contract** for later features (ADR-0047 point 10): on the member's click only, it reads the page in front of them (its address, its title and the selected text) and sends that to exactly one kernel command the feature names, run as the member in that org, tenant-checked, audited and tainted. Meetings does not use it.

**Later uses (an idea, not a commitment).** Other apps may add features to the shell through that contract, for example Ludy Engage bringing a lead or a conversation from a page into Hộp thư. That is for the Engage line to plan; no Engage feature is planned or built by this plan.

### 5.7 The extension's threat model

The extension runs in the member's browser, beside their other tabs, so it is held to the least it needs. Ludy owns its review, permissions, updates and security.

- **Least privilege, no broad host permissions.** The base install asks for `storage` and `identity` and a host permission for the one Ludy address the member signs in to (`app.ludy.vn`, or a self-hosted address the member types, asked at setup as an optional host permission). Meeting capture, when turned on, adds `tabCapture` and `offscreen`, and no host permission on any meeting site. A later page-capture feature would add `activeTab` and `scripting` when turned on, and nothing that reaches a site in the background. Never `<all_urls>`, `tabs`, `cookies`, `history` or content scripts declared for sites.
- **No reading of pages without a click.** Tab capture and page capture work only on the tab where the member clicked, after that click; the extension never reads another tab and starts nothing by itself.
- **How it authenticates.** It never sees the member's password or the app's session. Sign-in opens the Ludy sign-in page in a normal browser tab for the cloud address and `localhost` (#4662: the app sends the tab to the extension's own `connected.html`, the one web accessible page, which checks the state and trades the code), and in a browser window (`chrome.identity.launchWebAuthFlow`) for any other self-hosted address or when the tab cannot be opened; the signed-in member approves "Cho phép tiện ích Ludy làm việc với tổ chức X" (with a fresh second factor where the member has one), and the extension exchanges a one-time code with PKCE for an **extension token**.
- **The token.** One org, one member, and the scopes of the features the member turned on. Meeting capture's scope: create a meeting with its consent record, get upload grants for the meetings this token created, finish the upload, and read those meetings' status (processing or ready, never their content), and, for a meeting the recorder opted into live notes (ADR-0052 L4a), send the audio pieces of its recording while it runs (`meetings.live.chunk`, the one command L4a adds; no new browser permission). A second scope, `agenda` (B2, #3166, [meetings-integrations.md](meetings-integrations.md) section 5.1), opens one route: the member's next calendar events (at most 5 starting within 30 minutes, title, times and join host only) from their own B1 cache; it is asked for only when the member chooses it, and a stolen token with it could at most list those few titles under the rate limits. No other route answers it, and every command it reaches runs as that member, so it can never do more than the member can. It is kept in the extension's own storage; the kernel stores only its hash. It expires after 90 days, is listed under the member's connected devices and revoked there in one click, and is void at once when the member is removed from the org or loses a feature's permission. Every use is an audit row. A stolen token can at most do the turned-on features' acts as that member, under the rate limits and the AI budget.
- **Code and updates.** All code ships in the package (Manifest V3 runs no remote code); dependencies are pinned and few; a release is built in CI from a tag and published to the Chrome Web Store from the owner's developer account (an owner action); Edge installs it from the same store. A store review or a policy change can delay a fix, so the in-app recorder and the upload keep working without the extension.
- **Recordings on the device** live in the extension's own storage only until they are uploaded, then are deleted.

## 6. Audio storage and the transcription provider

- **Storage.** Audio is a `kernel.file` of purpose `meeting_audio`, with a neutral name (`meeting-<id>.opus`); a kept video is a file of purpose `meeting_video` under its own key (below). File rows are visible to every member, so the bytes stored are **ciphertext**: the claim step encrypts the upload with the meeting's own data key (AES-256-GCM in chunks), wrapped under the deployment key like a channel credential. The generic file download refuses this purpose; playback goes through the meetings module, which checks the reader and decrypts. The original upload is deleted once normalized; the normalized audio (mono Opus at about 24 kbit/s, about 11 MB an hour) stays.
- **Video, only when the person ticks it** for a meeting: kept as recorded (repackaged by the sandbox, not re-encoded), encrypted under a second data key of the meeting, its **video key**, so it is shredded on its own date, before the audio. Without the tick, the video track of a capture or an upload is dropped in the sandbox and never stored. Playback of video is a `sensitive` read like audio.
- **A video uploaded as the audio (#3337).** A member may pick a video file in the audio field of the upload form. The upload then asks for `extract=audio`: the API seals the video under a throwaway key held only in memory, streams it back to `crove-media`, which extracts the mono Opus, deletes the sealed video, and seals only that Opus as the meeting's audio, measured and recorded like any audio upload. The video is never recorded as a file: it gets no row and no key of its own, and the sealed temporary copy is deleted when the request ends (on presigned storage the browser's staged copy is deleted at the same point, or expires after 5 minutes if the browser never posts). A file with no audio track is refused (`NO_AUDIO_TRACK`); one that takes longer than the deadline, `MEDIA_CALL_TIMEOUT_MS`, is refused with `MEDIA_EXTRACT_TIMEOUT`. The container check accepts MP4 and QuickTime with a leading `ftyp`, and WebM/Matroska.
- **Media sandbox.** Checking, splitting and transcoding run in a new container, `crove-media` (ffmpeg only, no network, no secrets, read-only, CPU, memory and time limits, the hardening of `crove-extract`), never in the API or the worker. It extracts the audio track of a video, and answers normalized audio segments of at most 10 minutes, so every provider's request cap holds.
- **Provider boundary.** Transcription goes through `packages/agent` like every model call: the OpenAI-compatible `/audio/transcriptions` request, a deployment setting for the model and optionally its own base URL, so a self-hoster points it at a self-hosted Whisper-class server (for example whisper.cpp's server, a faster-whisper based server, or vLLM) and audio never leaves the org's machine. Vietnamese fine-tuned Whisper models exist; their licences and quality are checked in step 4. Whether the AI SDK's OpenAI-compatible provider covers transcription, or `packages/agent` needs a small client, is checked in step 4 too. The summary uses the existing text model.
- **What leaves.** The provider gets the audio segments, the language hint and, since #3499 (ADR-0047 point 5 as amended 2026-11-16), a limited list of names: the recorder's and the attendees' display names, the linked party and its people, never another party, a phone number, an email address, a tax code, an address or an amount. Names of customer staff are personal data of people who are not members, so this is part of what the lawyer (#62) reads in section 7. A provider outside Vietnam is a cross-border transfer for the org (section 7); the deployment's sub-processor list names it.
- **The job runs as the recorder's Vy** (ADR-0049 (the member-scoped Vy, PR [#2572](https://github.com/DOS/Ludy/pull/2572)), stage B): the worker enters the member-scoped context of the recorder's `member_vy` agent for one run, with the recorder's rights at most and R1, never the system context; it reads only that meeting and the attendees' names, and ends when the recorder leaves. Its output is written as draft rows only the recorder reads (section 8). **One live run per feature (#3029, owner decision 2026-11-12, ADR-0049 decision 1 as amended):** another use of Vy (a summary of a meeting, a note summary) no longer cancels a transcription that is still running. A transcription of another meeting does not start while the recorder has one going (`TRANSCRIBE_OTHER_RUNNING`), so a transcript is never lost to a second recording; a summary waits only for the transcription of its own meeting (`TRANSCRIBE_RUNNING`). The recorder can stop their own run (`meetings.transcript.cancel`, a stop button on the transcript card, #3033), so a lost job does not hold their slot until it expires; starts and stops of one recorder run one at a time, so two meetings started at once never cut the first one off.

## 7. Consent under Vietnam's personal data law

Law 91/2025/QH15 on Personal Data Protection (in force 2026-01-01) and its Decree 356/2025/NĐ-CP, which replaced Decree 13/2023/NĐ-CP. Read through the repository's earlier survey ([payroll.md](payroll.md) row 21) and commentary only: **[commentary]**, the lawyer (#62) confirms.

- **Who is the controller.** The org that records. Ludy is the tool and, in Ludy Cloud, a processor. The org must tell participants; the product makes that step unavoidable and records it.
- **Who must be told.** Everyone whose voice is recorded: members, customers, suppliers, candidates, people joining remotely. Consent must be clear and freely given; silence is not consent; it can be withdrawn.
- **How the UI records it.** On the meeting row, before any audio is accepted: who confirmed, when, how participants were told (said at the start, in the invite, in writing) and the version of the notice text shown. The audit row records the act with codes only. The server refuses audio without it. During recording the timer and a red dot stay visible.
- **Withdrawal.** A participant who objects: the person pauses, or deletes the meeting; deletion shreds the audio at once (section 9).
- **Voice as biometric data.** The law names biometric data sensitive. Slice 1 keeps no voiceprint and does no recognition across meetings; whether a provider's per-meeting speaker separation is biometric processing is a lawyer question.
- **Cross-border.** A provider abroad needs the org's transfer assessment; a self-hosted model avoids it. The impact assessment exemptions for household businesses and small enterprises, and whether they reach Ludy as a processor, are already open lawyer questions (payroll.md row 21).
- **Not verified:** whether recording a private meeting needs every participant's consent or the org's legitimate purpose suffices; whether the confirmation of the recorder is enough evidence of notice. Both go to the lawyer.
- **Additional bot-free capture questions (2026-11-20 UTC / 2026-11-21 +07:00, [ADR-0055](../adr/0055-three-capture-paths-one-meeting.md) section 5):** five non-bot capture questions (read-aloud / chat-paste notice sufficiency for bot-free desktop capture, timing before first audio and recorder confirmation as evidence, recording calls organized by outsiders such as a customer's Zoom or supplier Zalo PC call, in-person laptop capture notice, and confirming Law 91/2025 + Decree 356/2025 vs Decree 13/2023) are appended to [#2585](https://github.com/DOS/Ludy/issues/2585) and [#62](https://github.com/DOS/Ludy/issues/62) and gate Slice 3 desktop capture.

## 8. Who reads a meeting

Proposed in ADR-0047 point 2, an owner decision (section 14, Q2):

- **Readers:** the member who recorded it, and the members named as attendees. The recorder may share it with the whole org (owners, admins and members whose scope covers the org).
- **Owners and admins** do not read a meeting shared only with attendees; they see that it exists (date, length, recorder) for budgets and retention, and an owner can delete it for a personal-data request without reading it.
- **Viewers, org-chart agents and the system context** read nothing. The worker reaches meeting rows only through named definers that return ids and dates (retention, purge); a member's processing job reads its one meeting in that member's own context (section 6).
- **Vy (option (a) of ADR-0049, the Platform PO's ruling of 2026-11-04):** the recorder's own Vy reads what the recorder reads. Its output on a meeting (transcript, summary, decisions, items) lands in the **recorder's own Vy result**: draft rows only the recorder reads. The recorder publishes it to the meeting's readers with one click (`meetings.result.publish`, the recorder's own confirmed command), as tainted rows while the AI text is unedited. Nothing goes straight to a subject-audience sink: no Vy run writes a row an attendee reads, a task, a mail, a notification with text or a proposal. A draft never published expires after ADR-0049's 30 days. Org-wide outputs (the team brief, digests, proposals others approve) carry no meeting content.

## 9. Retention and crypto-shredding

- **Three settings** (owner or admin): audio kept for 30 days after the transcript is ready by default (or deleted right after, 7 or 90 days); a kept video 7 days by default (or 30 days; never longer than the audio); transcripts, summaries, decisions and items kept 12 months by default (or 3, 24 months, or until deleted). A change applies to meetings recorded afterwards, so the period participants were told stays true. A meeting whose transcript is never ready (AI off, no model, the kill switch, a spent budget) still gets dates: when it is recorded, its audio and video dates are stamped from the recording time with the same periods (the shortest choice, 7 days, when the setting is "deleted right after"), and the dates move to the transcript's time only if it is ready before them, so no audio waits for a transcript forever.
- **Audio and video are shredded, not deleted.** Each meeting's audio key and video key live in mutable rows no login role reads. At the end of each one's retention, or when the meeting is deleted, its key row is deleted: the ciphertext in the bucket, its old versions and every backup of the bucket become unreadable at once. The objects are then removed as the store allows (Crove holds no permission to delete old versions; a lifecycle rule reclaims the space).
- **Text is deleted.** Transcript, summary, decisions and items are rows of the module, deleted by the nightly job at the end of their retention or with the meeting; a draft the recorder never published goes after 30 days (ADR-0049). The search over transcript lines (L2) keeps no copy of its own: its folded words are a generated column of the line, rebuilt on every update and gone with the row. They are in no WORM batch, no outbox payload, no audit fact (a search's audit row hashes the typed words) and no activity summary (ids and codes only), so database backups are the last copy, gone when the backups expire (30 days on the pilot, M1-7).
- **Backups and restores.** A restore brings back keys and rows: the date-based ones are shredded again by the next nightly run; a deletion before its date is written as a shred reference to the archive store and replayed after a restore (the mechanism of ADR-0017 §8, built here for meetings).
- **Derived items.** A task created from an action item is the org's work record and stays, with the text the person confirmed; its link then reads "Cuộc họp đã xóa". A summary saved into a note (notes step N10, #1679) belongs to that note.
- **eSign meeting-minutes envelopes ([#3811](https://github.com/DOS/Ludy/issues/3811), [#4531](https://github.com/DOS/Ludy/issues/4531), owner decision 2026-11-24).** Signed / completed `meeting_minutes` envelopes remain legal records under eSign's retention rules and are never deleted or retroactively hidden when the meeting is deleted or `text_retention_date` expires. Unsigned / in-flight `meeting_minutes` envelopes follow active meeting reader scope even for `created_by` (revoked if removed from `meeting_reader` or demoted to `viewer`) and are voided and erased when the meeting is deleted (`meetings.meeting.delete`, `meetings.meeting.owner_delete`) or when its text retention expires (`meeting_text_purge`, `meeting_replay_shred`).
- **The window, stated in the privacy notice:** audio and video unreadable at the moment of shredding; the plaintext upload removed from staging within one day; text gone from backups when they expire.
- **Org purge and export.** The purge deletes every meeting row and key of the org. The export carries the transcripts and summaries of meetings shared with the org, never audio or video; attendees-only meetings are left out, as private notes are, and each reader may download their own meeting before.

## 10. Budgets and the kill switch

- Two feature keys in `AI_FEATURES`, both counting against a `meetings` module budget: `meetings.transcribe` and `meetings.summary`.
- **The meter learns audio length.** A transcription call records the audio seconds next to the tokens, priced by a deployment setting per audio minute (no price in this plan). Before the call the meter is asked with the length the sandbox measured, so a long recording cannot overrun the budget (done for the background run, #2863 item 8: each chunk is admitted on its measured seconds times the audio price, and a chunk that would reach a hard-stop budget is refused before the provider call); refused, the meeting waits (`usage_deferral` `ai_budget`) and starts when the month turns or the owner raises the budget.
- **Caps** (proposed): 2 hours a meeting; an audio upload up to 300 MB; with video, up to 2 GB; the per-minute upload rate of the file grants applies. Video costs storage, not AI: only its audio is transcribed.
- **Kill switch:** the existing "Dừng AI" for the org, the `meetings` module or the whole deployment stops new transcription and summary calls; calls in flight finish; recording and storage go on (section 5.4).

## 13. Risks

- **Vietnamese accuracy.** Accents, crosstalk and phone audio lower quality; a bad transcript makes a bad summary. Mitigation: the eval set gates a model change; every item links to its moment so a person checks the source in one click.
- **Secret recording.** The product cannot prove participants were told; it records who confirmed what. The privacy notice and the terms put the duty on the org.
- **Tasks leak meeting content.** A task is org-visible; the dialog says so and only the confirmed title goes in.
- **A dependency on the member's Vy.** ADR-0049 (the member-scoped Vy, PR [#2572](https://github.com/DOS/Ludy/pull/2572)) is Proposed and not built; meetings needs its stage B (background runs, the `member_vy` catalog). It has one owner, the Platform PO, and is built once for notes N8, meetings and projects; steps M4 and M6 wait for it.
- **A result nobody publishes.** Attendees see Vy's result only after the recorder's click; a recorder who forgets leaves them with the audio only. The recorder's Home shows the waiting result until it is published or expires.
- **Cost of long audio.** The pre-call admission on measured length and the caps bound it.
- **A new container.** `crove-media` is one more image to pin, harden and test in CI; it keeps hostile media out of the API and the worker.
- **The extension is ours to keep safe.** A compromised release would run in members' browsers. The permissions of section 5.7, no remote code, CI-built releases and the owner's account are the controls; the store's review adds days to any fix.
- **Browser and store changes.** Chrome may change tab capture or the store's policy; Safari and Firefox users get the microphone only; the Zoom and Teams desktop apps stay outside until `Ludy Desktop` ships under [ADR-0055](../adr/0055-three-capture-paths-one-meeting.md) after Slice 1. The in-app recorder and the upload are the fallback for each.
- **Video multiplies exposure.** Faces, screens and much larger files; hence off by default, a separate key and a short default retention.

### Follow-up email (L3, #2590)

The recorder of a meeting with a shared summary clicks "Soạn thư cảm ơn". The dialog is the meetings module's own (the Email module has no compose screen yet): it hands off to Email compose once that screen exists (docs/plan/email.md E4 and E7), and until then `meetings.followup.send` queues the mail through the kernel mail queue (`source = meetings.followup`, `source_id` the meeting).

- **The draft** is read, never stored: a greeting, the lead-in, the shared summary with its decisions and action items (owner and due date), and a closing, in the meeting's language (vi or en). The facts are rebuilt from the **published** result at the send; a result unpublished or shared again after the draft was opened refuses the send (`STALE_FOLLOWUP`).
- **Recipients** are the meeting's readers that are members and the contacts linked to it (L1), each with an email address, at most `FOLLOWUP_RECIPIENTS_MAX = 20`. The recorder edits the list. An address that is neither a reader nor a linked contact is flagged and sent only when the recorder ticks it, one address at a time (`confirmedOutsiders`, enforced on the server). A repeat send to an address that already got the mail needs a tick (`confirmRepeat`). A suppressed address is recorded as suppressed and never sent. At most `FOLLOWUP_SENDS_PER_MEETING_PER_DAY = 3` sends per meeting in 24 hours.
- **The AI part:** Vy words the lead-in and the closing only, told none of the meeting's content (no summary, decision, action item, name or date). The call goes through the AI budget meter (`meetings.followup_words`, meetings module budget) and the kill switches; a stopped, spent, failed or refused call leaves the fixed words. Vy's text is tainted: the recorder ticks "Tôi đã đọc" before the send, and the server checks every text the same way whatever its origin (no figure, link or address, not empty, not too long). With AI off the fixed words are the draft and no model is called.
- **A person sends:** the send is an R2 command from the recorder's click, once per idempotency key. The audit names counts and ids only, never an address or a word.
- **Erasure (#3016):** the mail queue keeps a follow-up mail's merge data (the shared summary, decisions and action item owners) until 30 days after its final status. A meeting's own delete, the owner's compliance delete, the text retention purge and the archive replay of a shred each also set that payload to NULL (the mail table lets a payload only be dropped), in the same transaction; the mail row, its status and its counts stay, and a mail still queued (not yet taken by the worker) then fails to render rather than send the text of a meeting that is gone. The drop and a send of the same meeting share one lock, so a send running at the same moment either finishes first (its mails are dropped too) or finds the meeting gone. The audit row holds the count (`mailPayloads`). The personal-data erasure of a mention (`kernel.personal_data.erase`, #3020) does the same: when `crove_meetings.personal_erase` changed at least one row of a meeting (lines, results, items or title), the payload and the subject (the recorder can type meeting content into it) of that meeting's follow-up mails are set to NULL in the same transaction, under the same lock (only tried, never waited for: the erasure already holds the meeting's rows and the delete commands take the lock first, so a busy meeting answers the retryable `PERSONAL_DATA_BUSY` and the whole erasure rolls back); the guard of the mail table lets both columns only be dropped, and the tenant role holds UPDATE on exactly them, so no migration is needed. A meeting where the erasure changed nothing (a mention it may not redact, kept and counted) keeps its mails, and other meetings' mails and other sources' mails are untouched. The audit row of the erasure holds the count (`follow_up_mails`). **By the mail's own text (#3025):** the same erasure also drops the payload and subject of every follow-up mail of a meeting the owner reads whose own subject or payload mentions the value (`crove_kernel.text_mentions`), whether or not the meeting's rows changed (the recorder edited the value out after the send, or typed it only into the subject), under the same try-lock (busy: the same retryable `PERSONAL_DATA_BUSY`, the whole erasure rolls back). A matching mail of a meeting the owner cannot read stays, counted in the answer's kept list (`crove_meetings.followup_mail`), because dropping a queued mail would destroy another member's pending send unannounced; the recorder's or the owner's delete of the meeting, the text retention purge and the archive replay drop its payload and its subject at once (#3063; the subject, which the recorder can type meeting content into, no longer waits for the retention worker's 13 months). A mail from before that, whose payload is gone but whose subject remains, fails to render when still queued, so erasing the same value again drops it whatever its meeting's readability. Otherwise the payload expires 30 days after the mail's final status. The mails are selected again by their meeting once the lock is held, so a send that committed after the scan is dropped too. The answer also says, when it dropped the copy of a mail already handed to its recipients (status sending, ambiguous, sent, delivered, bounced or complained; not a queued, failed or suppressed one), that such a mail cannot be recalled (the erasure drops Ludy's copy, not the copies in mailboxes; kept entry `crove_kernel.mail_message.payload`), and the finder's line for a recipient address says the same. An erasure made before #3020 cannot be backfilled (nothing records which value it was for or which mails it missed): running the same erasure again finds the leftovers. **Hardening (#3063):** a digit value of fewer than 10 digits (as for the activity redaction, `ACTIVITY_REDACT_MIN_DIGITS`) does not drop mails by their own text, since it may as well be a date or a figure in the payload: those mails stay, counted in the answer's kept list (`crove_meetings.followup_mail`, reason names the 10-digit limit). **Short numbers in the meeting text (#3064):** the same limit now applies to the meeting rows themselves: for a digit value of fewer than 10 digits `kernel.personal_data.erase` does not call `crove_meetings.personal_erase` (no migration, no change to the definer), so no transcript line, summary, decision, action item or title is redacted and, since no meeting row changed, the per-meeting mail pass has nothing to do either. The finder still reports the rows: the ones the owner reads are listed in the answer's kept list by column with the short-number reason, the ones they cannot read are counted by `crove_meetings.personal_matches` (read only, same timeout; kept entry `crove_meetings.text`, same reason); a value of 10 digits or more, and an email address, are redacted as before. The by-text scan runs under the meeting scan's statement timeout and savepoint (`meetingScan`): a timeout skips the by-text pass and says so in the kept list (the per-meeting pass still ran). A busy meeting (a send, a delete or a recorder's edit holding the lock) answers `PERSONAL_DATA_BUSY`, worded for an edit or a send. **The notice does not reach mails:** the notice and 24-hour route covers the meeting rows only; the follow-up mails of a meeting the owner cannot read stay, counted, and fall to the meeting's delete or the payload's expiry (decided, no change).
